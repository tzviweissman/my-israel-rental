## Improvement of the night

**Mechanism: automation bias, and specifically the commission error.** An operator
given an automated aid accepts its output without independent verification at a
markedly higher rate than they would accept the same claim from a colleague, and
the effect is strongest when checking the output requires *recalling* the source
rather than *comparing* against it. Skitka, Mosier and Burdick (1999) separate this
into omission errors (missing what the aid did not flag) and commission errors
(acting on what the aid got wrong); the commission error is the one that matters
here, and the standard mitigation in that literature is not "tell the operator to
check" but *making verification cheap enough that it happens*.

Not previously proposed here. The seven prior audits used the halo effect
(2026-09-01), the peak-end rule (09-02), loss aversion (09-03), denominator
neglect (09-04-2), endowed progress (09-05), cognitive fluency (09-06) and
ambiguity aversion (09-07).

**Surface:** the paste-to-fill path on the new order form, which is the headline
feature of `cba8884` and the thing the spec is built around: an owner pastes a
customer's WhatsApp message and eight fields fill themselves.

**File:** `frontend/src/components/dashboard/OrdersTab.jsx:610-637`, with the panel
markup at `:695-736`.

**What happens now.** `extract()` writes `customer_name`, `customer_phone`, `items`,
`total`, `date`, `time`, `fulfilment`, `address` and `notes` (`:616-627`). Then, in
this order:

```js
if (d.notes) setShowNotes(true);
setPasteOpen(false);                                    // :629
toast.success(t('orders.extracted', 'Filled in — check it, then save'));
nameRef.current?.focus();                               // :632
```

`:629` closes the paste panel, which unmounts the textarea holding the customer's
message. The instruction to check arrives in a toast that, per `index.js:69`, is on
screen for 1500 ms - the arithmetic from the 2026-09-02 audit applies unchanged.
And nothing on the form distinguishes the nine machine-written fields from fields
the owner typed.

So the owner is asked to verify eight values against a message that is no longer on
the screen, prompted by a sentence that is gone in a second and a half, with no
indication of which fields are the machine's. That is a recall task wearing the
costume of a comparison task, and the literature is clear about what people do with
those: they accept.

The stakes are not symmetric with a normal form error. `total` reaches the customer
on the track page (`OrderTrackPage.jsx:136-141`). `date` and `time` reach the
courier's run sheet and the kitchen's print-out. A Friday order read as Thursday is
a bakery's worst hour, and it is silent until it is not.

**What to change.**

1. **Keep the source on screen.** Replace `setPasteOpen(false)` at `:629` with a
   collapse to a read-only quote block above the form - the pasted text at
   `text-xs` in `--brand-muted` on `--surface-muted`, with a "Hide" control. The
   check becomes a comparison. This is the change that does most of the work.

2. **Mark what the machine wrote.** Track the keys `extract()` actually set in a
   `Set` in state, and give those fields a 2px `--gold` inline-start border that
   clears on the field's first `onChange`. Attention goes to eight fields instead of
   nowhere. No new copy, no new strings.

3. **Prefer empty over guessed for the two that escape the building.** If the
   backend returns a confidence signal for `total` or `needed_by`, leave a
   low-confidence value blank rather than filling it. A blank total is a question
   the owner answers; a wrong total is one they do not know to ask.

**What I would expect to see if it worked.** `source: 'whatsapp_paste'` is already
recorded on every such order (`:626`), so the comparison is available without new
instrumentation: **the rate of edits to an order within ten minutes of its creation,
and the cancellation rate, for `whatsapp_paste` orders versus typed ones.** If
paste-sourced orders currently show a *lower* early-edit rate than typed ones, that
is the commission error showing itself - the errors are there and nobody is catching
them - and the fix should push that rate *up* toward parity before the cancellation
rate falls. If the two rates are already at parity, this proposal is worth less than
I think and should be dropped rather than shipped on the argument alone.
