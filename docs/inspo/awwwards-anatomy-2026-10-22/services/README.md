Type: 4 of 5 use one sans (Truck'N Roll a very heavy condensed one); Aventura is the exception, a tall serif with italic words. Headings are huge (Truck'N Roll about 200 px, Aventura about 100 px). Colour: 1 accent only (blue, mint, none). Photo: real people and real equipment of that business, almost no stock; portraits in black and white (Aventura, Aspen) so the faces match. Density: airy on Aventura, packed grid on Aspen.

### Pattern
- 4 of 5 name the thing they do in the first sentence and show the real team or machines within two screens. None open with a mood film only.
- Services are shown as a short list of 3 to 6 named rows or panels, each with one picture and a "learn more", not as a paragraph.
- Trust is concrete: numbers (70+ trucks, 500+ placements), a list of real client names, the doctors' names and faces, two street addresses, local times.
- The button is always the same words, in the nav and again at the end: "Book a call", "Talk to a partner", "Plan your logistics", "Talk with us". It is a call or a quote, not a checkout. Quote form is short and says what to give ("best info you have now").
- Footer is a full-height closing screen with the call to action, address, phone and email.
- Difference from Moto Card: the ground changes by section type (dark for story, white for facts and numbers), not photo versus white; photos are of people and work, not glamour shots; 1 typeface, 1 accent; far less full-bleed.

### Pattern
- 4 of 5 are light (white, cream or yellow) with black type; only 2 use black and only for a short closing section. Photos are of finished rooms or buildings, shown small and many, never as the page background except the first screen.
- 1 sans or 1 serif at huge size; caps for section titles (Dado, Shed). Colour is a single flat accent (rust, blue, yellow) in blocks, never in gradients.
- The services or sectors are named in big words and each gets a picture; the project list matters more than the service explanation. A plain list of past clients or projects with place and size is the proof.
- Process is told with drawings or sketches (Kononenko), not stock icons.
- Contact is quiet: address, phone, email, newsletter box in the footer. No price, no booking widget; the invitation is "Work together?" or "Contact".
- Difference from Moto Card: the picture is the portfolio, not the mood; light ground nearly everywhere; much more text on screen; and text and hairlines do the structure that dark panels do on Moto.

Pattern: 3 of 5 repeat one black button with the same words at the top, the middle and the end; prices (when there are any) sit in 3 plain cards with one highlighted; a "Book a test ride / Book a call" link lives in the footer and the nav, never only in the middle. Small steps numbered 01 to 06 with one sentence each. Startups are the most like Moto Card (dark, big images) for hardware and the least for software (white, one illustration).

## Map to our businesses

KasherMyBnB (kitchen kashering for rentals)
1. Take the trucking model: say plainly what you do in the first line, show the real kitchen with the real person doing the work, in black-and-white or in one consistent grade.
2. Show the process as 3 to 5 numbered named steps, one picture each (Townhouse 01 to 06, Truck'N Roll "(1) (2) (3)"), with what the owner has to prepare.
3. One quote or "book a kashering" form that says what to give ("the best info you have now: address, dates, kitchen photos"), with the same button words in the nav and at the end.
4. A number block on a white ground (kitchens done, rabbis, cities) and a plain list of named places, in the style of the client name list; keep it truthful.
5. Light ground for facts, one dark section for the story; one typeface, one accent.

Noa Levi Home Styling (home organising)
1. Take the architecture/interior model: a light, nearly white site, with finished rooms as a photo grid of 6 to 9 (Studio Dado); sector or room tiles (Kitchen, Bedroom, Children, Wardrobe) as flat colour blocks each with one photo, like LPAS.
2. Before and after pairs as the proof; caption each with room and hours of work, as Kononenko lists the size of each project.
3. The process as drawings or plain photographs (a list, a sort, put back), not stock icons.
4. Quiet contact: phone, WhatsApp and a short "tell me about the room" form; footer with area served; no checkout.
5. Her own face and name large, as the doctors' portraits on Aventura; one accent colour.

Local service businesses in general (cleaners, movers, plumbers, tour guides)
1. The first screen names the service and the city and has the one button in the nav; real photo of the team or van, not a mood film.
2. 3 to 6 named services as rows or panels, each with one photo and a "learn more", and the price or from-price where honest.
3. A number block and a name list for trust (jobs done, years, streets or buildings served, customers who allow their names).
4. Footer as a full closing screen: the button, phone, address, hours, WhatsApp.
5. Movers and tour guides: a 3 step quote form like Truck'N Roll, and a map with the two or three places they work.
