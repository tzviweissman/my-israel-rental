# Page anatomy: events, sports, real estate (Awwwards, 22 Oct 2026)

Captured 22 Oct 2026 at 1440x900 and 390x844 (home page, screen by screen) plus one inner page that books or sells. Each `<site>-sheet.webp` is the contact sheet: home screens top to bottom, then the phone screens and the inner page along the bottom. The `single-*.webp` files are single screens that show a pattern best.

Honest note on "Sites of the Day": Awwwards has very few Sites of the Day per category (about 2 in events, 2 in sports, 5 in real estate), and several are agencies, a luxury-brand campaign or a driver's personal brand. So I used every SOTD that is a real business or product, then filled up with Honorable Mentions and Nominees that are real businesses, labelled below. Skipped: fighterready.com (a Nominee; its page hung the browser and never drew), Lacoste Ace Breaker (a brand campaign), Realevate and Likova (agencies). Capture problems: the Performance Lab pop-up and cookie bar cover part of its screens; Kortrijk Xpo's cookie wall stays on every screen; Belgrade Arbor and Monarque hold their screen while the scroll catches up, so some screens repeat or show a loader on phone; Sobha's phone page did not scroll in the capture.

### Anatomy
- **OFFF Forum.** One film screen (event photos, one centred serif line, one small white "Get your forum ticket" pill), then a white strip (newsletter sign-up, links), then a giant logo as the footer. The whole page is about 1.5 screens. Almost all picture on the first screen. Two families: a high-contrast serif for the line, a chunky sans for the logo. Black, white and the photo's own colour. Date and place sit in a small boxed table top left. Inner page (media room) is plain white, big black label heading, facts in two columns.
- **No Art.** Black cover with a handwritten logo, white rest. Sections: cover with a short "about" paragraph, big uppercase statement, dated event cards one image each with prev/next, a world map showing where events happen, shop row (5 tall product photos with name and price under each), link-grid footer. All text in a small capital sans. Only black and white; colour comes from the event art and the clothes photos. Shop photos are lifestyle: a person standing in front of the road cases, same spot every time. The shop page opens on a row of 5 product photos with price and a cart button bottom right.
- **Monarque.** Burgundy opening with small photos of real events (band, hands plating food, flowers) floating round a serif line "Orchestrer l'inoubliable" and one small button "Plan your event". Then navy statement with the founder's portrait sliding in, then stone-grey ground for services (4 plain text columns), a single-venue section (big name, photo, capacity, "Reserve the room"), client logos, a burgundy-and-orange closing call to action, and a giant name footer. One serif family, capitals for headings, 3 colours (burgundy, navy, stone), the ground changes per section, but the photos are small, not full-bleed. Airy, little text.
- **Boom Ball.** Night photo of a batter with a big sans heading and a pink pill button, then a cream ground for the rest. Sections: what it is, the show's moments as big pink condensed words, gold testimonial panel (4 fan quotes), "meet the teams" with a giant cut-out photo, navy "fun starts before the first pitch" block with photo, newsletter, giant logo footer. Three loud colours (pink, navy, mustard) on cream, condensed capitals, bright and playful. Inner Tickets page is the pattern that matters: a list of rows, each with date, city, ground address, a "More info" outline button and a pink "Enter the lottery" button; sold-out rows are greyed with "Sold Out" in place of the button.
- **Kortrijk Xpo.** Flat red hero with a pattern and "when ideas need space", then white sections each with one red sloped shape, a "coming soon" row of 3 event photos, explanation blocks, a huge red banner line repeated, news list. One rounded sans, lowercase headings, one colour (red) on white. Typical venue site: it organises information (calendar, visit, organise), not mood. Contact page is a plain sentence and one outline button to a form.

### Pattern for events
- 3 of 5 (OFFF, No Art, Monarque) open on real photos of past events; the other two open on a strong flat colour. None opens on a product shot.
- Most of the page is NOT picture. The pattern is: one loud opening, then a quiet ground with small photos and lots of type, then a giant name as the footer (4 of 5).
- The ground changes by section in Monarque and Boom Ball (cream, navy, mustard, burgundy) but stays one tone in Kortrijk Xpo and OFFF.
- One main button, always the same wording, in the nav and the first screen: "Get your forum ticket", "Tickets", "Plan your event", "Enter the lottery". Dates, city and place are shown as plain rows, not as pictures.
- Trust comes from past work: a row of client logos (Monarque), fan quotes (Boom Ball), a map of where it has happened (No Art), a named venue with capacity (Monarque, Kortrijk).
- Versus Moto Card (dark, full-bleed photo alternating with white rest): only the opening matches. Events sites are shorter, use flat brand colour, and sell by listing dates and past events, not by large product photos.

Sections: credentials row (logos of teams worked with), "what it is" paragraph, four services each with a photo and a "Learn more" button, a black proof block with giant numbers ("100+ NCAA commitments", "Olympic programs consulted"), method steps, testimonials with named people and titles, blog, FAQ, a "Let's talk performance" contact page. One grotesque sans, large capitals for display, sentence case for body. Ground alternates black and white. Credentials and numbers carry the trust.
- **Reel Motion.** An app page: a pale blue-lilac gradient ground for the whole page, one rounded sans plus one italic serif word, centred headline, phone mockups, three feature cards, "how it works" in three steps, three price cards side by side ($4.99 weekly, $19.99 monthly, $59.99 annual with a "best value" tag), a blue closing block with a QR code, dark footer. - **Sixes.** Maroon opening with the booking form right on the first screen: a cream card "Book party cricket" with Quick book / Event enquiry tabs, venue, date, time, people. Then a photo grid of what you can do, "Food and drink" big heading, a strip of venue circles, an "every occasion" green block with 4 photo cards, a "what's on" row, a counter of games played, and a giant pink "PARTY CRICKET" footer. Colours: maroon, cream, pink, green, blue, loud and changing section by section. Big condensed capitals. Photos are lifestyle: real groups laughing with a bat. The Book page is the same booking card.

### Pattern for sports
- The sale is on the first screen in the booking sites (Sixes puts the form in the hero; Boom Ball and Nick Ho a button) and a price grid in the app (Reel Motion). Nobody makes the visitor scroll to find how to book.
- Type is big, capitals, heavy sans (3 of 4). Only the app uses sentence case.
- Colour: 3 of 4 use one accent on black and white (yellow, red-orange, black); Sixes uses 5 loud colours. The ground alternates by section in all four.
- Photos are people in action or in use, often cropped tight; none is studio on white. Two sites (Nick Ho, Performance Lab) lean on proof: logos, numbers, named quotes.
- Versus Moto Card: the black-and-white alternation is the same, but a sports site fills the white part with numbers, logos and the booking tool, not more full-bleed photos.

### Anatomy
- **Belgrade Arbor.** Cream loader, then a deep forest-green ground for the whole page. Sections: a statement with cut-out photo shapes, a second statement with interior photos, giant serif "A new chapter in prestigious living" with a link "Explore our apartments", an "Explore in 3D" bar, and an inquiry page where a blurred photo fills the left half and a three-tab form (request, callback, business) is on the right. One condensed serif, capitals for big lines, green plus cream plus lime. Only 4 or 5 pictures; the type does the work. Button: "Inquire", a pill in the nav on every screen.
- **Sobha.** Black film opening ("The art of the sublime"), a corner "3D map" card, then it turns white for "of the rarest luxury homes" with a large photo that grows to fill the screen and two flanking photos. Only black, white and the photos. Serif italic plus capitals, tiny nav. Almost nothing to read: a campaign page, not a listing page.
- **ERA Residence.** Full-bleed photo hero (pool, white buildings, sky) with a script-and-capitals logo, then pale sky-blue and cream grounds that change per section, a "three reasons" arch, a "real-life location" block, a "new golden mile" photo block, a route line showing distance to the airport, and an aerial photo. The inner page "Apartments 25" is a filterable grid (type, bedrooms, sort) of floor plans, each with completion date. Three colours (navy ink, sky blue, cream) with magenta flowers from the photos. Condensed serif capitals. Mid amount of text; the facts are numbers and plans.
- **Coquelicots (one holiday house).** White ground with big rounded blob shapes in blue, green, red; the owners' own photo in a circle ("We fell in love with the Dordogne"), an aerial photo of the house and pool in blob frames, guest reviews as little cards with a score (10, 9.6), and the booking calendar on the page itself ("Book here directly"), then a red closing blob with a giant name. A rounded friendly sans, sentence case, a script tag over each heading. One dark-blue "Book" pill always in the nav. The contact page is a short form, the address and a route link. Warm, small, personal, not luxury.
- **Loam House.** Dark film hero with a registration pop-up, then one full-screen photo per selling point, numbered 01 to 06, each with a short serif line ("A backyard oasis, without the mowing"), the sizes in big numbers (112-156 m2, 2-3 bedrooms), a site plan with numbered dots, an aerial of the neighbourhood, and "Register your interest". Three buttons everywhere: brochure, private appointment, call back. Serif with italic, warm dark ground, almost all picture.

### Pattern for real estate
- Two families. The luxury projects (Belgrade, Sobha, Loam, ERA) are dark or deep colour, large serif, mostly pictures, very little text, a single "register / inquire" pill. The small direct owner (Coquelicots) is light, rounded and personal.
- One "next step" at all times: Inquire, Register your interest, Select an apartment, Book. 5 of 5 keep it in the nav, always visible.
- The thing being sold is broken into numbered or named parts (rooms, apartments, reasons), each with its own screen or card, with real numbers: size, bedrooms, completion date, distance, score.
- Maps and plans matter as much as photos: floor plans (ERA), site plan (Loam), distance line (ERA), 3D map (Sobha, Belgrade).
- Versus Moto Card: the big photos are the same, but the white rest holds facts, plans, a calendar or a form, and the whole page is more often one tone than a dark/white alternation (Belgrade green throughout, Loam dark throughout).

## Map to our businesses

### Michal Balloons (balloon decor for events)
Follows the events pattern, closest to Monarque and Boom Ball.
1. Open on real photos of her own jobs (an arch, a table, a birthday room), not a studio balloon; one calm line and one button, "Ask for a quote" or "Plan your event", also in the nav.
2. Keep the page short: opening, a short row of 4 event types as text columns with a small photo each (birthday, brit, wedding, baby), her past events as a dated list or photo strip, a few parent quotes with first names, a giant "Michal" as the footer.
3. Colour from the balloons themselves: cream ground, changing per section like Boom Ball or Monarque (two or three of her real balloon colours), not per product.
4. Show trust the events way: where she works (a list of cities or halls), how many events done, how far ahead to book. Add a visible "date taken / date free" line like the sold-out rows on Boom Ball.
5. Do not treat it as a shop: no price grid of single balloons; sizes and packages as rows with a "from" price, like a ticket row.

### Michal Yodaiken (women's personal trainer)
Follows the sports pattern, closest to the Performance Lab (a person's service) and Sixes (booking).
1. The booking is on the first screen: a small card (type of session, day, time) or one clear "Book a first session" button plus a WhatsApp button, in the nav the whole way down.
2. Large heavy capitals for the opening promise, one accent colour on black and white (the Performance Lab and Nick Ho model); real photos of her training clients in action, tight crops, never studio on white.
3. A proof block: numbers (years, clients, sessions), 3 to 4 named client quotes with a first name and what changed, and her certificates in a row.
4. The offer as 3 to 4 cards, each with a photo and a "Learn more" button (one to one, small group, online, pregnancy or after birth), and prices side by side as 3 cards like Reel Motion, with a "best value" tag only if true.
5. FAQ near the end (where, how long, what to bring, women only), then a short contact form with her phone and area.

### MyIsraelRental vacation and long-term listings
Follows the real estate pattern, closest to Coquelicots (one house, direct booking) and ERA (a list of units with plans).
1. Every listing page has a booking calendar or an "Ask about these dates" form on the page, plus one pill "Book" or "Ask" in the nav, like Coquelicots and Loam House.
2. Break each listing into numbered parts with real numbers: rooms, beds, size in metres, floor, distance to the sea or the synagogue, price per night or month, the way ERA lists completion date and type.
3. Put the owner or manager in as a person (a round photo and a first-name line) for vacation homes; add real guest review cards with a score for stays. Use a plan, a map and a distance line, not only photos.
4. For a building with several units, use an ERA-style filter grid (type, bedrooms, sort) with one card per unit. Keep the ground light and warm for vacation homes, calm and dark only for premium long-term rentals, one tone throughout.
5. Keep the text short: one sentence per room, big facts, big photos. Do not copy the Dubai or Melbourne luxury language; copy the structure.
