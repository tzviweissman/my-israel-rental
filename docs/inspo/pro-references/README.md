# Pro references: what makes them stop the scroll

The ads every one of ours is put beside before it is shown (scripts/ad_vs_pros.py, rule A9.5). Cropped from
docs/inspo/pinterest-ads-2026-11-02/best-of.jpg. Studied 8 Nov 2026 with the rest of that sheet and the five ad
videos (docs/inspo/ad-videos-2026-11-07).

What they share, and ours lacked:

1. **One idea you get in a second.** A visual pun or surprise: biryani in a ring box ("Will you... get biryani
   with me"), the painting reaching out to spray, a crocodile at the dentist, a pencil that is a rocket. The picture
   asks a question, the words answer it.
2. **Type and picture interlock.** The word sits behind and in front of the subject: SKATE, GET MOVING, VISA with
   the hourglass in it, HELLO with children climbing the letters. The headline is part of the picture, huge.
3. **Scale as a story.** A giant product and tiny people, or a giant word and a small subject.
4. **One colour world.** The background takes the product's own colour (the maroon Costa cup on maroon beans,
   biryani on maroon), with one accent. Ours put a yellow sticker and a white button on every ad.
5. **A frozen moment.** Beans falling, hair blowing, a skater mid-air, spray in the air: something happening.
6. **Light that sculpts.** One strong side or back light, a dark falloff, real depth of field.
7. **Three things, no more.** Headline, picture, logo. The call to action is small or absent; whole corners are
   empty. Ours carried a name, headline, sticker, button, link and QR code all at the same weight.
8. **Short, spoken headlines in two voices.** Bold condensed capitals with a script line under them ("KEEP GOING /
   Stay fueled"), 2 to 5 words, often a question or a pun.
9. **A hand or a person** using it, where the business has one (we use only real ones).
