## The one transferable idea
A static ad has three parts only: a scroll-stopping image, a headline that gives the reason, and a CTA that says what to do. Win with the words of a real customer (a review snippet or a story-style caption), not brand-speak. Copy mined from reviews sounds like the buyer, so it does not look like an ad.

## Techniques shown
1. Review quote as the headline, in a comment bubble (protein.com, "over six figures", top 1%, ranked 3 of 435 on Trend Track). Quote "Absolutely love these shakes. The weight is dropping off." in a white speech bubble top-left with a small round customer face and a red heart at the bubble corner; product jar and glass fill the picture; yellow/cream whole picture; bottom CTA is a small yellow label with black text and thin dark outline, "SHOP PINEAPPLE COCONUT FLAVOUR". Frame-verified: `frame-protein-review-ad-full.jpg`, `frame-protein-cta-circled.jpg`.
2. Name the specific variant in the CTA ("pineapple coconut flavour") because the brand's data said that flavour was rising; they tested other SKUs and this won. Frame-verified (CTA text); the testing claim is narration only.
3. Colour that stands out on a white or dark feed ("it's very yellow"). Frame-verified (the whole picture is one warm yellow family). Narration says statics sit at the bottom of the funnel: people already know the brand.
4. Native storytelling format: the image itself is a plain, shocking close-up (broken eczema skin, a photo of a hand), with no text on it at all; the whole story is in the long caption, whose first line ("A customer shared this with us the other day...") acts as the headline. Frame-verified: `frame-eczema-native-story-ad-with-caption.jpg`. Cheat for pictures: Google Images for the type of shot, then use it as a reference in Nano Banana to make a different image (narration only).
5. Native story-style ad (Humantra, CTR about 3.5%, six figures, made in 10 to 15 min): a phone photo, vertical, of the product held in a hand (POV, gray carpet floor), with 4 white rounded text boxes in Instagram-story text style stacked to the right, quoting the customer in first person ("No more 3pm crashes / No more brain fog", "3 weeks of Himalayan Lime and honestly the difference is mad..."). "@humantra" is underlined so people try to tap it; the text is added in Figma, not generated. Frame-verified: `frame-humantra-story-ad-full.jpg`, `frame-humantra-copy-closeup.jpg`. Underline-tap trick is shown in a circled frame; the CTR number is on screen as "CTR: 3.5%".
6. Headline from a social feeling, not a feature (Shay & Blue perfume, "claim it before the group chat does"): lilac background, bold navy sans headline top in 2 lines, glass bottle centred with lilac flowers and green gooseberries (real fruit matching the scent name), a one-line evergreen CTA at the bottom, "TAKE THE LEAD WITH OUR LILACS & GOOSEBERRIES 10ML." with the product name in magenta. No price, no discount. Frame-verified: `frame-shayblue-group-chat-ad.jpg`, `frame-shayblue-headline-image-cta-labels.jpg`. "First static a strategist made, became top performer" is narration only.
7. Mine reviews by hand: read the product page reviews, take a direct quote for the top of an image, one review gave "three concept ideas" in 2 minutes. Copy a review verbatim onto a photo = story static. Narration only (the review page is shown on screen in the sheet s03 but the example copy is read aloud).
8. Test the words and picture separately: take POV photos of the product, write many copies, combine; do not generate the picture with text baked in by AI, so you can change the copy in a Figma template. Narration only.
9. Evergreen CTA beats offer CTA: not "buy now, on offer", so the ad runs as long as the product is in stock. Narration only.
10. Variations: one message (the group chat) made into different-looking statics (call-out ad etc.) so Meta treats them as different assets. Narration only.

## Compare with our ads
Ours: canvas-drawn headline (2-8 words), the owner's cut-out product on one-colour ground with soft shadow, round yellow price sticker, short link + QR footer, square/feed/story, English and Hebrew.

Differences:
1. Their copy is a real customer's sentence (review bubble, first-person story text). Ours is a headline we write; we do not show social proof.
2. They look like content people post (comment bubble, story text boxes, phone photo in a hand) so they pass as organic. Ours are clearly designed ads.
3. Their picture carries a scene (pineapple, coconut and gingham cloth around the jar; fruit around a bottle; a hand holding the sachets). Ours is a cut-out on a plain ground.
4. Their CTA is a short action line tied to one named item; ours is a price sticker plus link and QR.
5. They show no price at all; the picture and headline do the job. Ours lead with the price sticker.
6. Colour: one dominant colour family in picture and text (yellow; lilac). Ours is a single ground plus yellow sticker, close to this.
7. Story-format ads: 4 stacked text boxes on a real photo, which we do not have as a layout.

Could adopt in a free, code-drawn ad:
- Review bubble layout: white rounded box, small circular initial or avatar, name, red heart, quote text, placed top-left over the photo. Needs a "customer quote" field (owner pastes a real review; we only use real ones).
- Story-style text-box stack on the owner's own photo (white rounded boxes, left-aligned, first person), plus underlined @handle/link in the last box.
- CTA label that names the specific item ("Order the pineapple tray"), small, yellow with dark outline, instead of a generic one.
- An "evergreen" no-price variant of each design.
- Background colour picked from the product photo's own dominant colour (one family).
Needs generation or a photographer: scene dressing around the product (gingham, fruit, props), POV hand-holding-product photos (owner can take these on a phone; no generation needed, only a prompt telling them how), the shocking close-up pictures, generated variants of the picture.
