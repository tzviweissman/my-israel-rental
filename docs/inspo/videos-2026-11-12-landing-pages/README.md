# "I Studied 1000 Landing Pages, Here's What Works in 2026" (12 Nov 2026)

Arsh Sanwarwala, ThrillX agency. https://youtu.be/PcwuZJNJny4 (16:45, Sep 2025).
Method: full auto-captions via yt-dlp, plus 20 frames pulled with ffmpeg.

**The one idea:** imagine the visitor reads only the headings. Each heading
must say the benefit by itself.

## The seven lessons

1. **The first screen does most of the work.** He puts the share of visitors
   who never scroll at 60% (his own figure). His recipe for that screen is:
   - a headline that names the result the customer wants;
   - a subline that says what you do and why you're different;
   - two kinds of proof;
   - one button;
   - a line under the button that answers the main worry (free trial, returns,
     warranty);
   - a picture that shows the offer.
   Frame-verified: the bookkeeping example says "Save 10+ hours a week & let
   us handle ALL finances for you", with stars, three client logos, then a
   row of figures ($75k+ saved a year, 45 hours a month). The air-con example
   puts "Rated 4.9/5, 224+ reviews" with the Google mark above the headline,
   and "Response in <5min · 10-year warranty" under the button.
2. **People decide on feeling, then justify it.** Show happy people using the
   product, not the product alone. Frame-verified: a tanning shop went from a
   "SALE SALE SALE" banner to a smiling woman holding the bottle. For services
   he uses problem, then make it sting, then the fix.
3. **A long page is fine if every section sells.** Never hide reviews in a
   phone carousel or a fold-out: "what is hidden will not get seen". On
   phones they show reviews stacked and fully open.
4. **People scan.** Use headings, bullets and bold. Ban empty headings such as
   "How it works", "Our services" and "What makes us unique". Frame-verified:
   "Make smarter & faster decisions with clear & timely reports" with three
   ticked lines.
5. **Proof must be checkable.**
   - Logo walls no longer persuade. Frame-verified: one shop loops the same
     three logos.
   - Add the outlet's actual quote under each logo. Frame-verified: Farmer's
     Dog under TODAY, Fast Company and Vogue.
   - A review needs the person's name, a photo and where it was left (Google
     and so on).
6. **Clear order of importance.** Headline biggest, then the subline, then
   one strong button. Too many choices means no choice.
7. **Plain beats clever (Jakob's law).** Use the layout people already know.
   Frame-verified closing wireframe, top to bottom:
   - logo and button;
   - proof;
   - headline;
   - subline;
   - main button;
   - worry-reducers;
   - picture or video.

## What to discount

- Every uplift ("+80%", "+132%", "+282%") is his own agency's claim, with no
  test details. Treat them as sales figures.
- "60% never scroll" and "images 60,000× faster" are stated, not sourced. The
  60,000 figure is a well-known myth.
- The video ends by sending viewers to his next video and agency.
- He's selling to ad-traffic landing pages. The advice suits lead-gen and
  shop pages more than an editorial brand site.

## What it means for us

Already in our rules:
- reassurance beside the button (3b.2);
- proof where the worry is;
- the category look (3t), which is Jakob's law under another name;
- the ban on "As seen on" logo walls and invented counters.

Gaps worth proposing. Each is an option until Tzvi approves it, and live sites
are never changed:
- **A. Headings say the benefit:** a check that flags generic headings such
  as "How it works", "Our services", "About us", "What makes us unique" and
  "Why choose us" on new builds.
- **B. Reviews never hidden on phones:** no carousel or fold-out for real
  reviews.
- **C. Each review shows the name and where it came from** (Google, Facebook,
  our site), only when the owner provided them.
- **D. A real rating near the top:** a Google rating with its count goes near
  the headline, only when it's real.
