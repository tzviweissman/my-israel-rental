# Nate Herk, "I Told Claude to "Make it better" 100 Times (It Went Too Far)"

youtu.be/DByAR5Su1Yw, 13:50, 899K views.

How it was read: the transcript panel in Chrome, plus 4 frames of the Opus site (all saved here).

## The one idea

"Innovate without a vision turns into adding." Told 100 times to "make one bold change", both models kept adding:
Opus 5.5 reached 21,000 lines, 54 AI images and 3 videos for about $137, and by round 100 it had broken its own
sections, a Spanish switch that did nothing, and a pumpkin-carving toy on a coffee shop site. "The model sets the
ceiling and the prompt sets the direction."

## Mechanics

- The prompt each round: "Look at the site first, then innovate. Think about the user experience and think outside
  the box. Make one bold change that improves conversions or the look and feel, not a small tweak. Use any tool you
  have. You must ship a change every time." Without "must ship" it stopped at round 20 to 25.
- A clean room every run: no skills, no CLAUDE.md, no memory.
- Sonnet 5.5: about 2 hours, $22 to $30, under 3,000 lines, first AI video at round 24. Opus 5.5: 8.5 hours, $137,
  first video at round 7.
- **Neither model ever searched the web** for inspiration, though both were told to think outside the box. He was
  "shocked".
- Both pushed ordering too hard: "a hard sell rather than more of like a soft close."

## The few good finds on the way (frame-verified)

- **Send the order to your phone by QR** (round 15, `DByAR5Su1Yw-qr-send-order-to-phone.png`): on a laptop, the
  order ticket shows a QR code. Scanning it opens a text to the shop with the order already written. He liked it:
  "that's actually not bad."
- **"Find your drink"** (round 32, `DByAR5Su1Yw-find-your-drink-map.png`): a map with two axes ("wake me up" to
  "something easy", "sweet and cozy" at the bottom). You drag a cup onto it and the nearest real drink shows on the
  right with its two sizes and prices and Add buttons. "One of the innovation times where I actually would have kept
  that change."
- **Reviews that show what each person ordered** (`DByAR5Su1Yw-reviews-with-their-order.png`): each card has the
  item, its price and an "Order what they had" line. We do not know whether those reviews were real; on his test site
  they were almost certainly made up.
- A swipe-cards menu (`DByAR5Su1Yw-swipe-the-menu.png`): a gimmick for a coffee order.

## What to ignore

- The ScrollCraft skill giveaway (a different "ScrollCraft" from our `scrollcraft/` folder) and the $1M agency funnel.
- Running any loop 100 times: the lesson is the opposite.
- Made-up stars, made-up "only two left" and made-up queue lengths, which our honesty rules forbid.

## What it means here

- Our restraint rules (3ai, three decorations at most; 3al.8) and forced references (REFERENCES.md, 3aj.1) are the
  cure he describes. Good to see from the outside.
- Two finds suit our order pages, and both can be honest because **our own reviews come after a real order**
  (rule 3 (cont.) item 5 and the order flow): we know what the reviewer bought.
- The two-axis chooser is the same family as Delta's coffee quiz, already named in 3ad as a signature body.
