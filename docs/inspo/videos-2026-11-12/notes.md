# Two static-ad videos, 12 Nov 2026

Tzvi: "what can we learn from these videos to make our ads better". Read from their transcripts (the first through
YouTube's transcript panel, the second by yt-dlp captions). Nothing here is adopted until Tzvi picks it.

## 1. "The Most Valuable Static Ads Training You'll Ever Watch", adswithsimon (Static agency), 15 min
https://youtu.be/WjoTTa2Sd8A

- **Angle before design.** Changing the headline, the picture or the colour of an ad is not a new test; it is the
  same idea said again. A new angle is a new reason to buy, from what the customer wants or worries about.
- **The reader recognises themselves.** Each winning ad speaks to one kind of person through their own world:
  gaming stats for men who played games, a podium for men who want to win, a kid's handwriting and "the iPad" for
  parents ("the best way to target parents is to target their kids").
- **Ads that do not look like ads** break the pattern of polished ads: a selfie, a notebook to-do list, an
  Instagram story. AI made polish cheap, so the native look stands out now.
- **A story in a text carousel** (several frames of plain words) for a hard angle.
- **The loop: research, make, test, read the results.** A winner is built on (same story, other format, or same
  format, other story); a dead ad is not revived with small changes, its angle has reached everyone it will. Write
  down each round's conclusion so the next batch starts from what was learned.
- An ad running a long time in Meta's Ad Library is a good sign it works (not proof it is profitable).

## 2. "Static Ads Masterclass: My Complete Process for 2027", Fraser Cottrell (Fraggle agency), 35 min
https://youtu.be/MIC7ZAvqjnE

- **The eye reads top to bottom in three parts:** the heavy words at the top (the hook), the product in the middle,
  the action at the bottom.
- **The action carries a benefit:** "Click for next day free delivery", not just "Shop now".
- **The headline mirrors the reader:** "Want a flatter stomach but can't be bothered with another detox?" Someone
  who is not that person scrolls past, which is the point ("creative is the targeting").
- **Reviews are the source of the words and the people:** read them for who buys (nurses, chefs, the person who
  wants to stop drinking) and the phrases that keep coming up; use the customer's words, never the brand's.
- **One or two points an ad,** never every selling point at once.
- **Formats he expects to win:** headline, us versus them, before and after, review, call-out, founder letter,
  post-it note, Instagram story, Instagram "ask me anything" Q and A box with the product as the answer,
  statistics, whiteboard (for a business with no product to show), collage, press snippet.
- **Judge a static by clicks** (click-through rate), then cost per result. A low click rate means a weak action or
  no offer.
- A person checks every ad before it goes out.

## What this means for our ad maker

Already in place: native designs (chat, post, notification, note, checklist, search), us versus them (versus),
before and after (split, torn), review, call-out (situation, mirror), headlines from the listing and its reviews,
a person checks (the owner picks and edits).

Gaps, each a candidate:
1. **Clicks per ad.** Every ad points at the business's one short link, so nobody can tell which ad brought the
   customer. Give each saved ad its own link (or a tag on the short link), and show the owner, per ad, how many
   people opened it: the "read the results" step of both videos, and it tells us which designs win.
2. **Angles, not one idea.** The AI writes one set of words; each design now gets one of its four headlines, but
   they are mostly the same angle. Ask for three angles, each a different kind of customer from the reviews (who,
   their want or worry, their words), and let the gallery group the designs by angle.
3. **The action with a benefit.** "Order Cholent kit" becomes "Order today, ready for Friday", from the listing's
   own facts (delivery, pickup, ready times); never a promise it does not make.
4. **Two new native designs:** the Instagram "ask me anything" box with the product as the answer, and a
   handwritten notebook to-do list.
