## What is worth taking

**1. The product you can turn (video 3, frames).** `turn-it-over-hotspots.jpg`.
"Turn it over. Look closer.": a 3D model of the product, made from one photo,
that the visitor drags to spin, with small dots on the parts that open a line
about each one. Earlier on the page the same model turns as you scroll past the
selling points. The model was generated (Higgsfield 3D, two candidates, about
44 to 62 credits by the narration, not checked here). For us: a shop with one
hero item (a board, a cake, a gift box) gets its real product to pick up and
look at, and each dot says a fact the owner gave (the hashgacha, what's inside,
the size). Must be made from their photo and compared beside it (rules 3c,
"one look per product"); a phone needs a swipe, not a drag-only control.

**2. Numbers that move (video 3, frames).** `count-up-chart.jpg`,
`mode-tabs-numbers.jpg`. Big figures that count up with a line that draws as you
reach them ("4,505 ft of climbing on one charge"), and tabs (four riding modes)
that change the numbers in place. For us: only real numbers the owner gave
(boards made, pieces in a box, minutes to deliver, years). Tabs that change one
panel in place suit businesses with sizes or packages, and agree with 3s.27
(same thing, same place).

**3. Side-by-side choices with bars (video 3, frames).** `compare-cards.jpg`.
Two models as two cards, each with its picture, price and the same four bars in
the same order, then one booking form listing both prices. For us: sizes and
packages (rules 3j and 3l), only where an owner has two or more.

**4. A light that follows the visitor (video 2, frames).** `flashlight-hero.jpg`.
The opening photo is dark and a soft light follows the mouse to show it; a small
button turns the effect off. On a phone the video only showed the layout, not
what replaces the mouse. For us: one possible "unique touch" for a business
whose world is dark (a night tour, a bar, a candle shop), with a slow light that
sweeps by itself on a phone and on reduced motion. 8a.4 bans cursor trails for
decoration; this would be an exception only where the light is the business's
own world, like the cloud pointer.

**5. Words that light up as you read (video 4, frames).** `words-light-up.jpg`.
"We build websites that feel like places you walk into": one sentence, the words
going from grey to white as you scroll. Cheap, all code. Our rule against
light-up STEPS is about lists of steps; one sentence that lights is different,
but it is very common on AI sites, so at most one per site and never twice in a
row across sites.

**6. A small brand kit before the site (videos 3 and 4).** Both build a short kit
first: colours, fonts, logo versions, a few generated textures in the brand's
colours, all saved in the project, approved, then used everywhere. Video 3 also
makes three cheap hero pictures first, asks which one, and only then spends on
3D and film, saying the credit cost at each step. That matches our build order
(stills first, cost before generating, 0b and the cheap-film rules); the kit as
one saved file per business is new for us.

**7. Watch the in-between frames (video 3).** The one fix he made: a shadow
under the 3D bike "just abruptly appears" between two frames. Same lesson as our
4 fps checks; nothing new to add.

## Second look: structure and details (Tzvi: "look at structure and as much details as you can")

Frames every 3 s through each finished site (about 220), the builders' own text on screen, the 3D site's

### The e-bike site, section by section (video 3, frames 19:46 to 22:20)

1. **Menu bar:** logo left; four page links; one solid "Book a test ride" button right. A thin progress
   line runs down the right edge the whole way, filling as you scroll.
2. **Opening:** full photo, headline lower left ("Built for the climb."), one line under it naming the
   product, the town and what you can do, then a solid button and a quiet text link. `opening-photo.jpg`.
3. **Scroll scene:** the product alone on black, turning as you scroll. Short headlines come in on
   alternating sides ("90 Nm. Barely a whisper.", "One bike. Pavement to pine.", "75 miles. Every climb on
   the map.") with one line under each. The last one carries the button. `chapter-lines-alternate.jpg`.
4. **One big number:** the figure counts up while a line draws to it. `count-up-chart.jpg`.
5. **Modes:** a heading ("Four modes. One button."), tabs, two figures that change in place, and a close-up
   photo card captioned "Sealed for Sierra weather." `mode-tabs-numbers.jpg`.
6. **A full-width film** of the product in use, scrubbed by the scroll, with a small heading.
7. **Turn it over:** drag to spin, dots on the parts, and three light buttons ("Shop light", "Night ride",
   "Alpenglow") that change the light on the model. `turn-it-light-modes.jpg`.
8. **Two models side by side**, with the same bars in the same order. `compare-cards.jpg`.
9. **The numbers:** a plain table of specs, with a switch between the two models.
10. **Booking:** a heading and one reassuring line ("Forty-five minutes on the bike... No charge, no
    pressure."), both prices listed on the left, and the form on the right: name, email, phone, model as
    chips, a time-of-day choice, and an optional note. Address, hours and phone sit under the prices.
    `prices-beside-booking.jpg`.
11. **Footer:** the headline again, huge and faded, then four columns (Shop with the address and
    Directions, Hours, Contact, Follow) and a small base line. `footer-faded-headline.jpg`.

**What the builder itself said it left undone** (its "Things to know"): the booking form has no server and
opens an email, the specs were made up and marked as placeholders, and one scene was changed. Our rules
refuse made-up figures and require saved orders, so that site would not pass ours.

**How it asked for the brand first** (Phase 1 of 6): 11 questions in one message (slogan, who it is for and
the price range, personality in a few words, colours with hex codes, fonts, logo, the product's specs, the
benefits to show, the visual mood, the site's main goal, contact details), then "skip what you don't have
and I'll mark it as a placeholder". It asked for a real product photo. Then six phases, each needing a "next".

### The dive site (video 2)

Menu of six pages plus "Book your dive"; the flashlight opening, with a small "Mode: reef light" switch in
the corner and a "Descend" cue at the foot; small vertical labels on the side edges; then an editorial
section with expedition cards and their prices; a month calendar of departures whose chosen date fills in
the booking form (the same idea as our pre-filled forms, 3s and check-prefill). The prompt asked for one
HTML file, icons as inline SVG, sizes from 1920 down to 380, and the light kept at 60 fps with
requestAnimationFrame and CSS variables.

### Jack Roberts' sites and rulebook (video 4)

- **A brief form that writes the summary as you fill it:** name, email, a line about the project, chips
  for what you want, chips for budget and timing, colour dots for mood; on the right, a live panel shows
  the request taking shape. `brief-form-live-summary.jpg`. For a service business (a caterer, a mover, a
  photographer) this turns a contact form into "here is exactly what you are asking for".
- **"Your favourite sites in DESIGN.md"** (Refero styles): a site's whole look written as one file. This is
  what BRAND.md (3x.7) is.
- **The web rulebook**, 20 rules with numbers. Most we already have (one main button per screen, the first
  screen answering what, where and what next, 44 px taps, phone checks, no layout shift, reduced motion).
  The ones our checks do not test yet:
  - every page has a skip link, exactly one h1, no skipped heading levels;
  - every button and link shows a clear focus ring (2 px, 3:1) for keyboard users;
  - form fields have visible labels, the right type and autocomplete (name, tel, email), and errors shown
    beside the field when you leave it;
  - the basket count and form errors are announced to screen readers (aria-live);
  - the opening picture loads first (fetchpriority high), the rest lazily, at most two font families;
  - the share layer: a title of 60 characters or less, a description of 155 or less, a 1200x630 share
    picture, and the business described for Google (JSON-LD LocalBusiness, with its products).

## Proposals (wait for Tzvi)

1. Add "turn the product" (idea 1) to the effects a shop site can pick, made from
   the owner's photo, with dots that say only the owner's facts.
2. Add counted numbers and in-place tabs (ideas 2 and 3) as options, only with
   real numbers or real sizes.
3. Allow the following light (idea 4) as a unique touch for dark-world businesses,
   with a phone and reduced-motion version.
4. Allow one lit sentence (idea 5) per site.
5. A saved brand kit per paid site (idea 6), before the first picture.
6. Add the galleries above to the sources every REFERENCES.md checks.

Items 1 to 6 became rules 3x on 4 Nov 2026 (Tzvi: "yes"). From the second look:

7. A web basics check on every site: skip link, one h1, focus rings, labelled fields with autocomplete,
   announced basket and errors, the opening picture first, the share layer and JSON-LD.
8. A brief form with a live summary, for service businesses whose order is a request.
9. Prices beside the booking form, so the price is in view while filling it.
10. Light buttons on a product to turn (shop light, evening), where the owner's item is shown in use.
11. A thin scroll progress line on long single pages.

## The 3D site's build file (CLAUDE.md, 81 KB, from the video's Notion page, read 4 Nov 2026)

Six phases, each ending on one question and a "next": a silent preflight, a brand kit file, the hero
still and the choice of experience, bringing it to life, the build, revisions, publishing. What we took:

- **Is it suitable for 3D?** Image-to-3D handles solid, opaque, chunky things and fails on thin or
  see-through parts (spokes, cables, glass, mesh, hair, foliage), people and places. It recommends the
  film for those, honestly, before any credit (3x.11).
- **The 3D source picture:** the same item, alone, whole, three-quarter view at eye level, plain mid-grey
  ground, even light, no props or floor reflections; a pure side view for long thin things.
- **Two 3D candidates** (Meshy 7 with textures and PBR, and Tripo), each reviewed on one page from four
  angles: outline matches the photo, the key detail is crisp, the back and base believable, nothing
  fused to the base, no smeared letters, not toy-like. Never ship a model you would not put on the
  thumbnail.
- **Hotspots** carry the direction their surface faces, so a dot hides when that side turns away; the drag
  layer keeps `touch-action: pan-y` or a phone cannot scroll past it.
- **Design bans** live in the brand kit and grow with every look the owner dislikes (its starting list:
  no cream background, no italic accent words in headlines, no "01 / 02" section labels, no monospace
  labels, no pill buttons, no gradient headline text, no emoji icons, no three identical cards in a row,
  no purple-to-blue gradients, no glass on every card) (3x.7).
- **A copy gate:** a search for long dashes and stock words (leverage, seamless, empower, unlock, robust,
  elevate, delve...) before showing. We already ban the long dash and stock phrases in 5.
- **Gotchas we had already hit:** a scrubbed film needs every frame a keyframe and a server with Range
  support (our 3h.22); a hidden preview pane pauses animation, so blank screenshots are not a broken site.
- Discount: it lets the AI invent specs and contact details as "placeholders", and sends the form to an
  email link. Both are refused by our rules (1, 3k).

## power-design-web (Jack Roberts' companion, read 4 Nov 2026)

Not a skill: one long page explaining the layers of a modern design system (tokens, shadcn, Tailwind v4,
Radix, OKLCH colour, the 8-point grid and a modular type scale, motion curves, theming) with its sources.
What helps our sites, and what we did with it:

- **One type scale and one spacing scale** (Bryn Jackson's 8-point grid; one ratio, 1.25, from the body
  size). Our sites showed 12 to 22 text sizes on a page. Now rule 3x.13, the starter's `--t-1`..`--t5` and
  `--s1`..`--s10` tokens, and check-web-basics SCALE.
- **Tokens first:** every colour, size and radius named once and used by name. Our starter and BRAND.md
  already work this way (3x.7).
- **Colour steps with jobs** (Radix's 12 steps: grounds, borders, solid, text) and OKLCH for even
  lightness: useful when a site needs hover and pressed shades of its accent; `color-mix(in oklch, ...)`
  derives them from one token.
- **Motion curves with meaning** (Material 3): standard 200 ms, emphasised 500 ms, enter 150 ms, exit 200
  ms. Our motion rules already set these ranges.
- **Systems worth studying** for how components behave: Vercel Geist, GitHub Primer, IBM Carbon,
  Material 3, Apple HIG, Atlassian.
- Not for us: Next.js, shadcn and Tailwind as a build stack, and dark mode toggles. Our business sites
  are plain HTML with one theme each.
