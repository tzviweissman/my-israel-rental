## What is worth taking

**1. The product you can turn (video 3, frames).** `turn-it-over-hotspots.jpg`.
"Turn it over. Look closer.": a 3D model of the product, made from one photo,
that the visitor drags to spin, with small dots on the parts that open a line
about each one. Earlier on the page the same model turns as you scroll past the
selling points. The model was generated (Higgsfield 3D, two candidates, about
44 to 62 credits by the narration, not checked here). For us: a shop with one
hero item (a board, a cake, a gift box) gets its real product to pick up and
look at, and each dot says a fact the owner gave (the hashgacha, what's inside,
the size). Must be made from their photo and compared beside it (rules 3c,
"one look per product"); a phone needs a swipe, not a drag-only control.

**2. Numbers that move (video 3, frames).** `count-up-chart.jpg`,
`mode-tabs-numbers.jpg`. Big figures that count up with a line that draws as you
reach them ("4,505 ft of climbing on one charge"), and tabs (four riding modes)
that change the numbers in place. For us: only real numbers the owner gave
(boards made, pieces in a box, minutes to deliver, years). Tabs that change one
panel in place suit businesses with sizes or packages, and agree with 3s.27
(same thing, same place).

**3. Side-by-side choices with bars (video 3, frames).** `compare-cards.jpg`.
Two models as two cards, each with its picture, price and the same four bars in
the same order, then one booking form listing both prices. For us: sizes and
packages (rules 3j and 3l), only where an owner has two or more.

**4. A light that follows the visitor (video 2, frames).** `flashlight-hero.jpg`.
The opening photo is dark and a soft light follows the mouse to show it; a small
button turns the effect off. On a phone the video only showed the layout, not
what replaces the mouse. For us: one possible "unique touch" for a business
whose world is dark (a night tour, a bar, a candle shop), with a slow light that
sweeps by itself on a phone and on reduced motion. 8a.4 bans cursor trails for
decoration; this would be an exception only where the light is the business's
own world, like the cloud pointer.

**5. Words that light up as you read (video 4, frames).** `words-light-up.jpg`.
"We build websites that feel like places you walk into": one sentence, the words
going from grey to white as you scroll. Cheap, all code. Our rule against
light-up STEPS is about lists of steps; one sentence that lights is different,
but it is very common on AI sites, so at most one per site and never twice in a
row across sites.

**6. A small brand kit before the site (videos 3 and 4).** Both build a short kit
first: colours, fonts, logo versions, a few generated textures in the brand's
colours, all saved in the project, approved, then used everywhere. Video 3 also
makes three cheap hero pictures first, asks which one, and only then spends on
3D and film, saying the credit cost at each step. That matches our build order
(stills first, cost before generating, 0b and the cheap-film rules); the kit as
one saved file per business is new for us.

**7. Watch the in-between frames (video 3).** The one fix he made: a shadow
under the 3D bike "just abruptly appears" between two frames. Same lesson as our
4 fps checks; nothing new to add.

## New places to look for ideas (video 1)

We already use Inspo, Mobbin, 21st, Awwwards and Pinterest. Worth adding to
REFERENCES.md searches, by what each is good for:

- **Godly**: pages shown as scrolling videos, so the motion can be seen.
- **Hover States**: short videos of what buttons and pictures do when touched.
- **Unsection**: single sections (hero, reviews, contact) laid out many ways.
- **Landbook**: search by section type.
- **Commerce Cream** and **Ecom design**: shops, shown on a phone first.
- **Maxi Best Of**: sorted by font pair.
- **Codrops**: how-to pieces for effects, with working code.
- **One Page Love, Minimal Gallery, Site of Sites, Killer Portfolio, CSS Design
  Awards, FWA, CSS Winner, CSS Nectar, Lapa Ninja, Landings, LandingFolio**: more
  galleries; Lapa Ninja and web-design-inspiration are full of adverts.
- **Refero styles** (video 4): a site's look written out as a style guide.

## Proposals (wait for Tzvi)

1. Add "turn the product" (idea 1) to the effects a shop site can pick, made from
   the owner's photo, with dots that say only the owner's facts.
2. Add counted numbers and in-place tabs (ideas 2 and 3) as options, only with
   real numbers or real sizes.
3. Allow the following light (idea 4) as a unique touch for dark-world businesses,
   with a phone and reduced-motion version.
4. Allow one lit sentence (idea 5) per site.
5. A saved brand kit per paid site (idea 6), before the first picture.
6. Add the galleries above to the sources every REFERENCES.md checks.
