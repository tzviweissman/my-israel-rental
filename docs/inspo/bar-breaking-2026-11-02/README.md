# Bars being broken, filmed for real (2 Nov 2026)

Tzvi asked for real videos of bars being broken, so the next generated break (Krysp) can copy how it
really happens. Found on Pinterest and YouTube; about twenty clips were watched as frame sheets at 4 fps.
Most were AI or CGI adverts (exploding chocolate, floating bars, a press), which show what not to copy.
Three real ones are kept here, each as a strip at 4 frames a second, read left to right.

## rice-treat-pull-apart.jpg — the closest to Krysp's inside
Source: https://www.pinterest.com/pin/676595544065644138/ (0 to 4 s)
- Two hands, thumbs on top, pull a crispy rice square apart over about 3 s. Nothing happens by itself.
- The gap opens unevenly: one side tears first, the other still holds.
- The strands are many, thin, of different lengths and at different angles, not an even row of threads.
- They do not snap back into the faces. They stretch, thin, and the longest still hang between the halves
  at the end. Grains stay stuck to them.

## hands-break-soft-bar.jpg — a dense bar torn by hand
Source: https://www.pinterest.com/pin/666251338659084927/ (2 to 5 s; GoMacro advert, a real shot)
- One hand holds each end; the bar bends a little, then tears from one edge across, about 1 s.
- The torn faces are ragged and match each other; the halves are then held a hand's width apart and stay.
- Plain white ground, soft light from the side; the hands are in the shot but only to the wrist.

## caramel-strand-sags.jpg — how a sticky strand behaves
Source: https://www.pinterest.com/pin/468796642486651042/ (0 to 3 s)
- A gloved hand lifts a piece; one thick ribbon of caramel stretches from it.
- Gravity pulls the ribbon down into a curve; it thins in the middle but does not spring back up.

## What this means for a generated break
1. Hands cause it, one at each end, in the start picture (rules 3w.1, 3w.8).
2. The tear starts at one edge and runs across, about 1 s; then the halves part slowly and stop.
3. Strands are uneven, some long, some short, and the longest still hang and sag at the end (3w.2).
4. The torn faces match; no crumbs appear that were not knocked off by the tear.

## ai-stretch-held-apart.jpg — liked by Tzvi (AI, "Neon Bite" advert on Pinterest)
Tzvi, 2 Nov 2026: "this one looks pretty good" (his screen recording; frames at 6 fps, first 2.4 s).
- The bar is already in two halves, held a little apart and still; the camera pushes in slowly.
- The caramel spans the gap as a web, not threads: thick at the faces, thinning in the middle, with
  uneven holes opening. The filling layer shows in both faces.
- Why it works: one slow event, nothing breaks on screen, so there is nothing for the model to get wrong.
- Not to copy: the sprinkles that then burst out of the gap from nowhere (3w.1).

## Four more AI adverts Tzvi sent (2 Nov 2026, "look at these too")
His screen recordings of Pinterest pins by "AI network visuali...". All generated; kept for the look of the web.

### ai-caramel-crunch-held-apart.jpg — the closest to Krysp (Cadbury Caramel Crunch, 5.2 to 7.2 s)
- The same idea as Neon Bite: two halves already apart, held still, the caramel web across the gap.
- The web has crunchy bits stuck in it, like Krysp's rice in peanut butter; it thins, holes open, one
  thick strand stays to the end. The torn faces show the crunch layer.
- Not to copy: crumbs flying upward from nowhere, and the hard cut to a bar in a caramel splash (3w.1).

### ai-crack-runs-then-web.jpg — the best order of events (Milka cookie, 2.4 to 5.4 s)
- A crack line shows first and runs top to bottom; then the halves part slowly and the chocolate
  stretches as a web pinched in the middle, with crumbs falling down (gravity, not upward).
- Not to copy: no hands, it splits by itself (3w.1); later chocolate ribbons fly in from nowhere.

### ai-cookie-web-holes.jpg — the web at its best, and what goes wrong (Oniro cookie, 7.6 to 10 s)
- The web is thick at both faces with big round holes between, crumbs stuck in it: the best web of all.
- Not to copy: the halves then close again and the web goes back in (3w.2, nothing springs back);
  a chocolate pour comes from nowhere before it.

### Not kept: the truffle and swirl clip
Floating truffles, swirls and fruit bursting in mid-air: nothing has a cause (3w.1).

### What the five liked adverts share
Not one shows the bar being torn in close-up. They start from the halves already apart, or a crack line
that only opens; the sticky web is the whole event; the camera hardly moves.
