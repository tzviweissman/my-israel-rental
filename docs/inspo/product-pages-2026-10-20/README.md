# Product and order pages, from Tzvi (20 Oct 2026)

Seven screen recordings of product-page designs and nine stills (a skincare
product page, a candle shop, a serum page with reviews, three restaurant and
bakery pages, a coffee shop, two water-bottle pages) that Tzvi sent for our
order and menu pages: "pull out different design techniques or psychology
methods they might be using". Watched as video (one frame per idea saved
here). Borrow how they work, never their look, and every idea still passes
the page rules (honest only: no invented reviews, no fake scarcity).

## The techniques

**1. The product IS the page, one at a time** (closca-1, closca-2, jacket-1, storehouse-1, food-1)
One product fills the stage: big photo, name in big type, price right under,
one button. Arrows, dots or a small row of the others switch to the next one
with a crossfade, and the page around it changes with it.
Why it sells: one decision at a time (less choice overload); the product is
seen big, so it feels real.
For us: the menu and the order become one page, each item its own stage.

**2. The choice recolours the whole page** (jacket-1, jacket-2, storehouse-1, closca-2)
Picking a colour or a product changes the background, the blob behind it,
even the type colour. The next option waits in a corner as a teaser.
Why: the visitor sees their choice instantly and big, which makes it feel
already theirs (the endowment effect), and it is fun, so they try more.
For us: picking Caramel turns the stage caramel; a nail shade already does
this on Elisheva's page (one per site; use with a business whose products
have colours).

**3. Each product lives in its own world** (closca-1 savanna / beach, bottle-2 snow, buds-3 snow and mud)
The product sits inside a scene that says where you use it, framed in an
arch or circle.
Why: people buy the moment, not the object.
For us: a jar on a sunny breakfast table, a cake at a birthday. Generated
scenery only, the product always their real photo (3o.1).

**4. Hotspot labels around the product** (buds-1)
Small "+ Features", "+ Colours", "+ Specs" labels float around the product;
each opens a short fact.
Why: answers questions where the eye already is, without a wall of text.
For us: on a cake or box: "+ Serves 8", "+ Kosher", "+ Delivered Friday".

**5. Product taken apart** (buds-2, bottle-1)
The product splits or bursts to show what is inside, then closes.
For us: only in code on their real photo layers (3o.1: no generated food
motion); suits gift boxes, boards, sets.

**6. Price with the old price struck through** (jacket-1, the serum still)
"$149 ~~$199~~".
Psychology: anchoring. Allowed only for a REAL saving the owner gives (a
bundle against single prices, a dated offer, rules 3c.7), computed by us.

**7. Proof next to the button** (serum still, candle still, skincare still)
Stars with the count ("4.8, 245 reviews"), "Best seller" tag, small trust
icons under the button (natural wax, free delivery over X, made in France).
Psychology: social proof and risk removal at the moment of deciding.
For us: only real reviews and the owner's real guarantees, beside the button.

**8. Size and quantity as chips, total in the button** (serum, candle, water bottle stills)
Sizes are pill buttons, quantity is − 1 +, and the button says "Add to basket,
28 €". Subscribe-and-save as a second radio line (skincare still).
Psychology: fewer steps, and the price is never a surprise.

**9. Thumbnails of the same product under the big photo** (skincare, candle, serum stills)
Several angles of ONE product, same light and background.

**10. Three feature cards, then FAQ, under the product** (bose-2, bose-3, candle still's icon row)
Four short facts with icons (burn time, wax, scent, made where), then the
questions customers ask, all on the product page.
Why: the doubts are answered before they leave to ask.

**11. "You might also like" row at the end** (skincare, candle stills)
Same-size cards, price and button on each.
Psychology: cross-sell after the decision, never before it.

**12. One promise line, very big** (bottle-2 "Drink it all in", bose-1 "We put more in. You get more out.", jacket "Stand out without trying")
Five words or fewer, the size of the product photo.

**13. A dish rolls in with its rating and maker** (food-1, food-2)
The plate rolls in from the edge, the name comes in huge, a rating badge and
"Chef Reza" beside it; a strip of dishes underneath switches them.
For us: their real dish photo cut out (cutout.py), the owner as the maker.

## Psychology, in short
- One decision per screen; the big photo does the persuading.
- The choice is shown instantly and big (it feels owned already).
- Price, proof and guarantees touch the button.
- Anchors only when true (old price struck through = a real saving).
- Answer doubts on the page (facts, FAQ) so nobody leaves to ask.
- Cross-sell after the main choice, not before.

## For our builds
Proposed (Tzvi, 20 Oct 2026: "they can probably be merged into 1"): Menu and
Order become one page, the shop, built from techniques 1, 2, 7, 8 and 10.

## Two store home pages Tzvi sent after (PureGlow before and after, AURA skincare)

Pictures not saved (sent in chat). What they teach:

14. **A thin bar of real promises above everything**: "Free shipping on
    orders over $75", "Complimentary samples with every order". Psychology:
    a free-delivery threshold makes people add one more item; a free sample
    is reciprocity. Only with the owner's real terms.
15. **A row of four promise icons right under the hero** (free shipping,
    natural ingredients, secure payment, support). Answers "can I trust
    this" before the products start. For us: kosher, made where, delivery,
    how to pay, from the owner.
16. **Category icons in one row** (cleansers, serums, masks...): one tap to
    the part of the shop you want. For a shop with several kinds of item.
17. **Two promo tiles side by side** (two collections, each photo, line and
    button) before the best sellers.
18. **Best sellers with a round "+" on every card**: add without opening the
    product, price and stars on the card. "Best seller" only from real
    orders or the owner's word.
19. **"As seen in" logos** (authority) and an **Instagram row** (real
    customers' photos): only real press and their own feed.
20. **"10% off your first order" for an email**: only an offer the owner
    really gives.
21. **The before and after itself**: the same sections in both; what made
    "after" better is one palette, one card size, every section a clear
    job, real photos big, and the promise strip. Consistency again.
