## For our pages

Shop page, product tiles
- Show one plain price per product: name left, price right on the same line, no sale badge on everyday items.
- Put colour or option swatches as small square chips (selected one ringed in black) with a grey "+4 more" when there are extras.
- Add a small outlined tag top-left of a tile saying what the item is for (e.g. "FINISHING OIL"), price top-right.
- Name each tier with a tiny caps word, a quoted name and one plain line saying who it is for ("Made for eating, never heating"), so the right buyer recognises it before reading the price.
- Show stars and the real review count next to the product name; show pack sizes as small grey pills and "from" price on multi-size items.
- Use a "Quick add" bar that appears over the photo on hover, and a small bag-plus button in the corner on touch.
- Use rounded pill filters (selected one outlined or filled dark) above the grid.
- Put a "Bestseller" tag on a photo only for a product that really is the best seller.

Packs, bundles and savings
- Show a bundle as the set price with "($X Value)" in grey beside it plus a small outlined "Save ₪Y" chip, only where X is the real total of the same items sold apart.
- Show a saving as the old price struck through beside the new one, plus a short "Save ₪X" tag; no sentence and no percentage headline.
- Describe a bundle by what it replaces ("13 piece, replaces 18") so it is weighed against the whole job, not one item.
- Keep the main product at one price all year; put discounts on kits and sets, and clear slow stock that way.
- Run at most two to four sitewide sales a year, each with a real start and end date ("Nov 24 - Dec 1") or a small round "Ends July 15" badge tied to a real event.
- Give price-sensitive buyers a separate "Seconds / Overstock / Clearance" section (lightly used, old price struck through beside new, plain "Sale" or "Out of stock" tag) so the main range keeps its value.
- Put a first-order offer inside the product box as a pale-tinted panel ("₪X off your first order") with two tick lines ("Skip, delay or cancel anytime"); nothing off later orders.
- Offer a new-customer deal in one small dismissible email popup: line of context, "₪X off your first order", fine print saying who it is for, optional preference chips, email field, one dark button.
- Mark the largest pack with a small "Best value" tab only when it truly costs least per unit.

Delivery, guarantee and trust
- Put a thin grey bar above the header with a threshold or guarantee, not a sale ("Free delivery over ₪X and easy returns").
- Put three small icon lines (13px) directly under the Add to cart button: the guarantee, the delivery threshold, the returns.
- Add a row of 3-4 short icon facts above the product gallery (warranty, trial, returns, delivery) with only real terms.
- Put the guarantee in the product grid as a coloured tile the same size as a product card, with a short heading and two plain sentences.
- Write the money-back or warranty terms plainly, including what is not covered, with specific examples and no vague wording; add "we may change this policy" only if true.
- State the catch in an FAQ accordion in plain text (membership, minimum term, who the warranty covers), with a chevron per row and thin dividers.

Proof
- Put one real named review (first name, initial, "Verified Buyer", the detail they gave) next to the section it answers; skip generic review walls.
- Add clickable question chips near the buy button for the two biggest fears ("Are they chlorine free?") that jump to the answer.
- Footnote a claim with the evidence behind it ("holds 16 oz, based on lab testing of size 4").
- Show certificates or test results as small round badges on the product photo, only real ones, with the issuing body named.
- If true, show a searchable list or map of real customers with city search, and a counter strip of real numbers with a "+" in the accent colour.
- State what you do not do as a headline when it is true ("We don't pay anyone to say this. They buy it themselves.") and caption each customer photo with a name and place.
- Show a real, dated running total when it is real ("We've raised ... to date").
- Show a real founder quote strip with name and role under it.

Booking and home page
- Say who the page is for in one plain line in the about text, in the customer's own words, and never say who it is not for.
- Grey out the main button ("Select a size" / "Choose a date") until the choice is made, then activate it.
- Add a "what to expect" timeline on the booking confirmation or page: pill labels (Day 1, Week 1, Month 1) with a heading and three bullets each, naming the awkward first step before it happens.
- Include a welcome card with the first order or first visit that explains the first week.
- Use a full-bleed autoplay video hero with the headline bottom-left, a small pill button, and a pause control on any autoplay tile.
- Use a numbered editorial list (big thin numeral, serif heading, short paragraph, one real quote, underlined "Shop now") for benefits.
- Set a story section in serif with a few italic words and one large photo (a short history or why it exists).
- Put a short quiz or "find your match" helper box next to option pickers ("Know your size? Pick above. New? Take the 2-minute quiz").
- Before writing copy, ask past customers what almost stopped them from buying and what they would be embarrassed to own, and write the page as the alternative.
- Do not discount best sellers; they sell either way.

## Not for us (dishonest or not applicable)

- 30% OFF banners, red "30% OFF" tags and "LIMITED TIME ONLY": the video shows these as the thing not to do (discount land, percentage-off framing, urgency).
- "UP TO 50% OFF" / "UP TO 60% OFF" / "SAVE UP TO $300" headlines and "10% off MSRP" tags: percentage-off framing; use money saved only.
- "Sellout Risk" pill: scarcity badge; only acceptable if it is real stock data, otherwise leave out.
- "30,000 5-Star Reviews" wall of identical quotes and big review counts: generic proof the video itself calls useless; no invented counts.
- Cart-abandonment email "15% OFF, code COMEBACK15, valid for 7 days" and lululemon-style "valid for 14 days": email-flow expiry pressure, not a page change; skip unless the expiry is real and the flow exists.
- Membership-required warranty and 12-month plan lock-in (Eight Sleep architecture): designed to build lock-in, not applicable to a small shop or booking page.
- Pay now / Rent monthly / Affirm / HSA tabs and sticky "Checkout" bar with finance: finance options not available to us.
- Agency self-promotion (free growth audit, logo marquee, "$100M+ revenue" strip, client carousel, "LATER IN THE VIDEO" teaser): the host's own marketing, not site content.
- Income or ZIP-code targeting and "attractive people in your ads": the site is for anyone with something to offer or need; do not write copy aimed at the rich.
- Stock footage of casual joggers as "who it is not for": excluding a group in copy conflicts with our owner-neutral, inclusive positioning.
- Donation and "50% of profits donated" style claims: only usable with a real, verifiable figure; otherwise leave out.
- Slide-system styling (green marker boxes, blur-to-sharp bars, lower-third captions): video production, not page design.
