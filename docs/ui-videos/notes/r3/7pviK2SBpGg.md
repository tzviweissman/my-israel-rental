## Easy to miss
1. Prices sit in a peach pill, regular weight, on every comparison card; the Aesop price repeats inside the button label ("$55.00 — Add to cart").
2. Selected option = 2px black border and bold; unselected = 1px light border (Aesop sizes, Tesla trims).
3. Aesop offers a free sample above $125 as a calm strip under the button and as a tile in the grid, not a discount or countdown.
4. Aesop tiles use a tiny brown label ("Beloved formulation") and a scent note instead of stars or sale tags; the size dropdown is visible on the card.
5. Momentous: one orange accent for buttons, pale-yellow one-word chips, a struck price in lighter grey beside the real price, green-tick benefit lines, photos of people breaking the product grid every second slot, black-and-white expert portraits, stats band with hairlines.
6. One phrase highlighted in yellow marker inside a long paragraph ("third-party testing") makes proof scannable.
7. The Black Stuff shows a real review count beside five stars on every collection card.
8. Anchoring demo dims, not removes, the other cards (to ~40%).
9. Lists reveal one line at a time, about every 2 s, at speaking pace.

## For our pages
1. Show a saving as the old price struck through in lighter grey beside the real price, with a short "Save ₪X" tag in money; no sentence, no percentage banner (Momentous card, Myprotein tag).
2. Put the price inside the main buy button label ("₪55 — Add to cart") so the price is never out of sight at the moment of buying.
3. On a size/pack picker use outlined boxes: the chosen one gets a 2px black border and bold label, the others 1px light; each box shows its own price.
4. Offer a refill or smaller size at a lower price beside the main one (Aesop $55 / refill $50 / small $38), only where the shop really sells them.
5. Under the buy button add one quiet strip with a small icon for a real perk (a free sample above a basket threshold, free delivery above a threshold); state the threshold in money.
6. Show each product card with one small label in tiny brown/grey caps ("Beloved formulation", "Best seller") only when true, plus a one-line grey description (scent, taste, use) instead of stars or sale tags.
7. Put the size/option dropdown inside the card, with a small floating "Size" label and the price under it, so it can be chosen without opening the page.
8. Use three green-tick benefit lines (~14px) on a product card, each a concrete fact about the product.
9. Use pale-yellow one-word chips top-left on cards ("New", "Best Seller", "Upgraded formula") sparingly and only when accurate.
10. Break the product grid with a photo or short silent video of the product in use every second or third slot, same radius and height as a card.
11. Show a real review count as a number beside five stars on collection cards (e.g. "5 stars 2614 Reviews") only with genuine reviews; leave it off if none.
12. Add a proof strip of real stats in quiet columns: hairline dividers, ~26 to 30px light-weight headline, ~15px paragraph, on a slightly darker cream band; no icons.
13. Highlight the one key proof phrase inside a quality paragraph with a yellow marker (#FFF200) and use a large (~60px) heading above it.
14. Show how it is made: process photos or short clips with burned-in captions (handmade, small team, cured for weeks), and say it in one line near the price.
15. Make the real quality difference easy to see in the first 3 seconds of an ad or page (ingredient, material, test, person who uses it).
16. Feature real experts, customers or locals who use the product: tight black-and-white portraits in a 3-column grid, name caption inside the tile, a 2-line bio under it.
17. Use an "as seen in" logo strip (white logos on a black band) only for press or partners that are real.
18. Show a real range of tiers so the middle option is clear: give a premium option its honest price and let the mid option sit beside it (anchoring, as ChatGPT, Tesla); do not invent a decoy.
19. Highlight the recommended option by dimming the others to ~40% or giving it the black 2px border and a bolder label, not by hiding the others.
20. For high-ticket or custom items, offer "Inquire now" that opens a pre-filled WhatsApp message with the product link, next to a normal "Add to cart".
21. Give an item that is genuinely limited (a batch, a season, a drop) a quiet label ("Only a few left") only when stock is truly low, in tiny letter-spaced caps (~9px) top-right of the tile.
22. Do not make the cheapest price the main message; lead with who it is for and why it is worth it (price is part of the perception).
23. Use a calm, near-text-free hero: one product or person, small caption, a thin 1px outlined "Discover more →" button (Aesop, Rolex) on the home page.
24. Cart panel: "Subtotal" larger and bold, black full-width "Checkout Now" button, payment-method logos right under it, one "Today's highlight" add-on row below (one product, price, "Add to cart" link).
25. Reveal list copy one line at a time, about 2 s apart, black square bullets, for any "what you get" list on a page.
26. Show a small announcement bar (black, white underlined text) only for a real, current offer or product news.
27. Use photos of real locals or creators wearing or using the product, with their consent, instead of stock models.
28. Read competitors' reviews to learn why people pay more, and put those real reasons in our headline lines.

## Not for us (dishonest or not applicable)
- Percentage-off banners ("UP TO 41% OFF BIRTHDAY SALE", "35% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING"): percentage framing, and a sale label that may not be real.
- "Was £29.99" tags (Myprotein) when the old price was never charged: fake reference price (only a real previous price may be struck).
- A decoy £300 item built only to make £100 look cheap: invented anchor.
- "Waiting list" or "limited" claims when not true: fake scarcity; the video itself says scarcity only works for products already wanted.
- Celebrity or MrBeast-style endorsement, or AI images of famous people holding the product: invented endorsement.
- Claims like "all 32 NFL teams trust us", "50,000+ men" or "2614 reviews" with no real source: invented counts.
- Discount-code stacking ("FRASER30", "20% off" UGC frame): discount framing, and the video says it lowers status.
- Sponsor read for Omnisend, agency QR/CTA, course plugs, "$70,000 month on ads" badge: promotion for the channel, not applicable.
- Meta ad-library research and paid-ad creative tactics (Brandtracker, studio ad copying): not applicable to a small shop page.
- Gucci/Rolex-style "no price, no button" pages: not applicable to a shop that must show price and basket.
