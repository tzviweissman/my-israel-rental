## For our pages

1. On a photo hero, put every overlay icon (back, save, menu) in its own solid white circle, ~36 px, 1 px pale-grey ring, same size and same top margin; test it on a dark, a bright, a busy and a plain photo.
2. Use one photo style and one off-white background (~#F4F0F0) for every product, cut out and centred, so a grid of products always looks consistent.
3. Show the product the way it is sold (a basket for something sold by weight, a pack for a pack), not a single item or a hands-holding lifestyle shot.
4. Fix one left margin (24 px on phones) and one right margin; badge, price, stepper and button all end on the same right edge.
5. Check text on the accent or button colour for contrast before using it (white on neon green measured 1.70:1); keep text on solid fills at 4.5:1 or better.
6. Colour roles: the image gets the colour emphasis, secondary elements stay neutral, one accent (the button) is used sparingly.
7. Use one typeface family and make hierarchy with size, weight, colour and line height; heading ~18-20 px bold, body ~13-14 px regular.
8. Small uppercase labels: add ~0.05em letter spacing and a muted colour.
9. Badges: short words only, sentence case, no icon (for example "Free delivery" or "Fresh today"); use a percentage badge only if a real earlier price existed.
10. Body text: line height ~1.5-1.6 and a slightly softer slate (~#4A4A5A) instead of pure black; keep descriptions to two short sentences in a friendly voice.
11. Put the rating row directly under the title (5 amber stars, bold "4.8", regular "(89 reviews)"), only with real counts.
12. Feature or benefit list: one row of four items, icon above a sentence-case label, outline icons in one colour, same stroke, evenly spread across the width.
13. Dividers: 1 px pale grey (~#E3E3E3), never black or 2 px; tighten gaps to equal ~24 px steps and group related items closer.
14. Never put the word "Price:" before a price; the currency sign says it.
15. Keep quantity out of the product title; show the unit beside the price ("₪62 per kg") and use two decimals everywhere.
16. Put the price high, directly under the rating, with the quantity stepper on the same row (stepper in a 1 px pale box, ~8 px radius).
17. Add to cart button: sentence case, ~14-16 px semibold, ~8 px radius, no shadow, never all caps.
18. Put the quantity stepper next to the add button in one bottom row, same height; show the unit inside the stepper ("1 kg").
19. Show the real total inside the button ("Add to cart · ₪62") and update it live as quantity changes; this removes uncertainty about what they will pay.
20. Make the page a card with ~24 px rounded top corners and a soft shadow that slides up over the product photo; the title moves into the top bar and stays sticky once the photo is gone.
21. Keep the quantity stepper and add button in a sticky bottom bar (white, rounded top corners, soft top shadow) on long pages.
22. After tapping add, fly a small cart icon toward the basket and turn the button into a green circle with a white check.
23. Offer quick-pick tiles for pack sizes ("Choose weight"): equal tiles with size bold, a short name in italic grey and the total price; selected tile gets an olive border; tapping sets the stepper and the button total; keep a custom stepper too. Order the tiles from real sales data.
24. Show an "Often bought with" 2-column grid under the details (same cut-out photos, name bold, price grey, small "+" quick add), using real products the shop sells.
25. Feature chips: soft grey pills with icon + label ("Free delivery", "Organic") instead of a loud icon row when space is tight.
26. Test any new product photo in a catalog grid next to the others before using it.

## Not for us (dishonest or not applicable)

- "20% off" red badge as the offer headline: percentage framing; show the saving in money instead, and only when a real earlier price existed.
- Struck-through "$7.20" next to "$6.20" as a design device: only honest if the old price was truly charged; never invent a "before" price.
- "4.8 (89 reviews)" on a page: only with real reviews from real customers; never invent counts.
- "Often bought with" and "30% choose 1 kg": only from real sales data; never made-up percentages.
- "Snack / Regular / Family / Party" tile names as nudges toward bigger sizes: fine only if each tile is a real, fairly priced option; no decoy sizes.
