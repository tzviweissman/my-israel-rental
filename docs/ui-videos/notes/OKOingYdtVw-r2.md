# Wyatt Feaster: "The hidden psychology trick that makes any app 10x more addictive" (second study)

- Channel: Wyatt Feaster. URL: https://youtu.be/OKOingYdtVw. Length: about 7:55.
- Method: video downloaded at 720p with audio and subtitles this time. 9 contact sheets at a frame every 3.5 s (all looked at), full-size frames of every designed piece, and a 2 fps strip of the loop diagram drawing itself. Transcript read only alongside the frames. First study notes: `OKOingYdtVw.md` (read first; its facts are correct, this adds what it skipped).
- Honest summary: about 70 percent of the runtime is the presenter on camera. The designed material is a few dark title cards, one animated loop diagram, four loop cards, and a run of stock clips and phone mocks used as proof for each spoken point. The subject (compulsion tactics) is mostly banned for us; the way it is SHOWN is the useful part.

## The four loop cards in detail (the first study listed contents, not design)

Same grid every time, so only the content differs:
- Near-black ground, a 1 px rounded outline around the card in the app's own brand colour (TikTok teal, Instagram pink, Snapchat yellow, Duolingo green).
- Top left: app name in a bold serif display face in the brand colour. Top right: a small grey pill "HABIT" plus a one-line habit ("Any spare moment to TikTok", "Post something, keep checking", "Every single day to Snapchat", "Before bed to Duolingo").
- Four corners, read clockwise from top left, each with a tiny grey numbered label ("1 TRIGGER", "2 ACTION", "3 REWARD", "4 INVESTMENT"), a short bold white plain-words line, and under it an italic serif line in the user's own voice ("I have a few minutes." "Okay, one more." "Maybe the next one's amazing." "It knows exactly what I like.").
- Centre: a thin ring in the brand colour with two arrowheads and four small icons (lightbulb, hand, award, database) in dark circles, cross-hair lines dividing the quadrants.
- The mechanic used is a small coloured pill under the line it explains: red "No stopping cues", yellow "Variable-ratio", green "Social reinforcement", orange "Loss aversion", blue "Zeigarnik". One colour per mechanic, the same on every card, so the key is learned on the first card and the other three read at a glance.

## Lessons

1. **Show the thing the moment it is named** (icons at 0:00, buffet clips at 1:55, laundry at 3:05, Snapchat list at 5:24). Never more than about 2 seconds of narration without a matching picture. For us: a section that names "our sourdough" shows the sourdough in the same screen, not three paragraphs later. NEW (3g and 3i cover big photos, not the naming-to-picture timing).
2. **Give each idea a one-phrase title card before explaining it**: one plain line, nothing else, about 2 seconds. Page equivalent is a section heading that is a full phrase and stands alone, with no label above it and no counter (3g already bans those). NEW, low priority.
3. **Write what the customer thinks, in quotes, under each step.** The cards pair a bold plain step ("Swipe up") with a small italic line in the user's voice ("Okay, one more."). For a "How it works" strip: step name in bold, then the customer's thought ("I need it ready for Friday."). Framed as a thought, never as a quote from a named person. NEW.
4. **A four-step loop with numbered clockwise corners reads in one glance**, provided the four corners are the same size and layout. Use only where the business has a real repeat cycle: Order, Confirm, Pickup, Reorder (caterer); Book, Visit, Result, Rebook (nails). NEW.
5. **One colour per meaning, repeated exactly**, so the legend is learned once (the five mechanic pills). For menus and price lists: kosher, gluten free, vegan, spicy each get one fixed small tag colour and wording on every item. Green stays functional only (CLAUDE.md). NEW (3e covers chip consistency, not a colour-per-meaning key).
6. **Identical card layout across several examples** lets differences pop and makes four cards feel like one product. ALREADY (memory: one look per product; 3g).
7. **A diagram must be readable at the size it lands.** The drawn loop at 5:44 has labels about 9 px tall on a 720p frame in light grey on charcoal; on a phone it is unreadable and the real information sits in the cards. Rule for any diagram or timeline we draw: smallest text at least 14 px on a 375 px phone and at least 4.5:1 contrast; if it cannot fit, make it a list. NEW (checkable with the readability script).
8. **Cards held still while the speaker discusses them (about 20 s each)** let the viewer read at their own pace. On our pages a menu or package card does not move or auto-advance while someone reads. NEW.
9. **Proof shown as the real artefact**: the praise is a screenshot of the actual chat, bubbles and all (3:56). For a small business with a real, permitted WhatsApp or Google review: a tidy crop of the real message beats a styled quote card. Never recreated or improved. NEW as presentation; the honesty rule is ALREADY (3b.5, 3c.5).
10. **A meme beat inside a serious point** (1:15, 1:25) works only as one short clip. Not for small-business sites. NEW, not adopted.
11. **Show the opposite as a mock to make a point** (the "Would you like to continue?" dialog, 2:28). Our version: "Save and finish later" on long forms. ALREADY (old notes 2 and 3).
12. **Urgency copy is the first thing shown in the video** ("Watch it before it ends!", 0:04), exactly what the `urgency` rule bans. Check no page, email or WhatsApp template of ours uses that shape. ALREADY (rules section 3 `urgency`, 3j "Never").
13. **A "habit line" per business**: the top-right pill states in one line when the customer uses you ("Before Shabbat", "After the gym", "Every Friday"). On a page it is one line under the hero heading or in About, supplied by the owner. NEW as a pattern.
14. **Reward and investment, honest forms**: reward is the real service delivered; investment is saved details and a Reorder button. ALREADY (old notes 7 and 8, 3j.5, 3c.10).
15. **Unfinished loops**: a "your order for Friday is not confirmed yet" message, sent once, only for something the customer started. Streaks, day counters and sunk-cost guilt rejected. ALREADY (3j.5, 3c.10).
16. **Pacing**: the presenter is never on screen more than about 12 s without a cutaway or graphic; cutaways run 2 to 4 s. Page version: a visual change at least every screen height, never two plain text blocks in a row. ALREADY in spirit (3g, 3n); the number is NEW but weak.
17. **The sponsor segment keeps the content visible** (QR in the corner while he carries on). Nothing to take. NEW, not adopted.
18. **The closing "not actually that harmful" claim has no example shown**, so no rule can be taken from it. ALREADY (old notes 9).

## Do not copy

- Variable rewards, random prizes, streaks, endless feeds, hidden stopping points, meaningless social counts, "watch before it ends" urgency, sunk-cost guilt, fake unread badges.
- The dark neon cards as a look: near-black with a coloured outline belongs to a tech explainer, not a bakery or nail salon. Take the quadrant structure only, in the business's own palette.
- The memes, the sponsor QR segment, the chalk diagram text size.
- Any claim that these tactics help "retention" without a limit; the first study's honesty filter stands.

## For our pages

- Explain ordering or booking in four short steps, each with a plain step name and one line in the customer's voice ("I need it ready for Friday."), same layout for all four. Fits caterers, meat boards, gift shops, bakeries with pre-orders.
- Show the picture the instant the name appears: a nail salon says "gel with removal" next to the real photo of that set, not in a text list.
- Use one fixed small tag colour per meaning (kosher, gluten free, vegan), identical on every item, so a menu scans in one pass.
- Show proof as the real artefact: a neat crop of an actual customer message or Google review, with permission, never an invented styled quote.
- Keep any process strip readable on a 375 px phone (14 px minimum, strong contrast); if it cannot, use a numbered list.
- Say once when people choose them (a habit line from the owner: "for Shabbat tables", "after work") and make the return path a Reorder or Rebook button, never a streak or countdown.
