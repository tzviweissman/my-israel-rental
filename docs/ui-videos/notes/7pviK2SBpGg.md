# Fraser Cottrell: "Why Some Brands Can Charge 10x More"

- Channel: Fraser Cottrell (paid-ads creative agency, ecommerce brands). URL: https://youtu.be/7pviK2SBpGg. Length: about 20 min (1202 s).
- Method: video downloaded at 720p and viewed as 13 contact sheets (a frame every 6 s, 4x3), full transcript read alongside (youtube_transcript_api; yt-dlp subtitles gave HTTP 429). Tags: SHOWN = on screen, SAID = narration only.
- Subject: brand positioning and price perception. This is a marketing-psychology talk about why a 3 pound soap and a 35 pound soap sell, not a UI video. Most of the screen time is the presenter to camera in a studio, with graphics (numbered chapter cards 1# to 8#, bullet lists, two-product price cards) and screen recordings of real brand sites (Aesop, Tesco, Gucci, Momentous, iHerb, Crafted, Carhartt, The Black Stuff, Rolex). It is about ads first and landing pages second, so only part of it applies to a small-business site.

## Lessons

1. **The page must say why it costs what it costs, in the same screen as the price.** Name the one real difference: made by hand, how long it takes, what goes in, who does the work. The customer sees a 3-second look, not the 2 years behind it. Check: next to each price or package, a visitor can answer "why this one?" without scrolling. SAID 13:05-14:20, SHOWN in the Black Stuff clips and Momentous "Certification". ALREADY partly (3c, "their story in their words", 3h "process"); the explicit "answer it beside the price" check is NEW.
2. **Show the process as a short real clip or labelled steps.** The Black Stuff burns two or three words onto real footage ("natural essential oils", "nearly five years ago") and cuts straight to the product grid. Builder rule: every business gets one 3 to 8 second real clip of the making with one line of text on it. SHOWN 14:20-14:50. ALREADY (3h motion, 3.1594 "motion shows the real physical process"); keep.
3. **Say who it is for and who uses it, with real people, not with a logo wall.** Aesop reads as a lifestyle because it sits in places the buyer recognises. For a small business that means real customers (named, with permission), real events, real venues, real photos of the product in use. Check: at least one image on the home page shows the product in a real setting with a person, not only a pack shot. SHOWN 1:20-1:50, 8:20-9:40. ALREADY (3b.5 real reviews, 3i real context photos); the "product in a real setting with a person" image check is NEW as a measurable item.
4. **Proof as big numerals with one plain sentence under each.** Momentous: "32 of 32 NFL teams", "15 expert partners", set as a large figure over small body copy; "Certification" as one large word over one paragraph. For us only real numbers (years open, orders filled, events catered, count of reviews). Check: a proof row shows 2 to 4 figures at least 3x body size, each with a one-line label, each one the owner can state. SHOWN 6:00-6:40, 13:30. NEW as a layout rule (3c.299 covers the content, not the figure treatment).
5. **A plain studio pack shot is enough once the page has built the context.** The top Momentous ad is a clean pack shot on a pale ground; trust did the work before. Builder rule: do not keep adding effects on a product row; give each product one clean, even-lit photo on a calm ground, the same crop and ground for every item in the set. SHOWN 7:15-7:40, Carhartt and The Black Stuff grids. ALREADY (one look per product, 3k Shop page); confirms it.
6. **Grids of near-identical items work when the frame is identical.** Carhartt jackets, Black Stuff soaps, Aesop bottles: same ground, same size, same crop, same type size, name and price below. Check: in any product grid all images share width and height, all captions share one font size, only the item changes. SHOWN 10:05, 14:30. ALREADY (one look per product); good reference set.
7. **Price comparison is context: show three options and make the middle one the natural pick.** SAID 15:50-16:30 (anchoring). ALREADY (3j.6 "the middle option", honest only: never invent an option to make another look good).
8. **The "Show the deal when it is real" rule lines up with anchoring.** A bigger size or pack shown against the smaller sizes' price is the honest form of the anchor. SAID. ALREADY (3j "show the deal", money only, never a percentage).
9. **Make expertise visible in the first screen.** Dr Sims talking about the product for 30 seconds was the fourth best Momentous ad; the site carries a podcast clip and named partners. For us: the owner (or the person doing the work) on camera or in a photo near the top, with name and role, for service and food businesses. Check: the home hero or the section directly after it shows the maker or the person who does the work, by name. SHOWN 7:45-8:10, 5:55-6:10. ALREADY partly (3i, their own words); the "named person near the top" check is NEW.
10. **Positioning is repetition across every touchpoint.** Packaging, site, photos, who is shown, the ad that led to the page. Builder rule: the link-preview image, the page and the Instagram highlight use the same photos and the same one-line promise, so the customer meets one brand three times. SAID 3:10-3:30, 17:30-17:50. ALREADY (3k.5 link previews, 3p no two alike); the "same promise line in the preview and on the page" check is NEW.

## Do not copy

- The hand-soap, wine, Rolex and Tesla examples are about brands spending years and large budgets to borrow status. A small business cannot buy celebrity or an NFL partnership, and must not imply one.
- Borrowing status from a famous person: never put a celebrity, an athlete, a brand logo or a "trusted by" wall on a page unless the owner really has it (page rules: claims need proof).
- Scarcity (limited colours, drops, "selling out early", Rolex waiting lists): fake scarcity and countdowns are banned (3j). Limited runs only when the owner really limits them and says so.
- Price anchoring by inventing a very expensive "flagship": banned by 3j.6 ("never invent an option to make another look good").
- The "20% off" discount codes, the kitchen-selfie ad and the "30% off Omnisend, code FRASER30" read: sales pitch and a promo code, ignore.
- The agency and course pitches at the end, the sponsor read from 11:46 to 13:00.
- Invented review counters or star rows. The Momentous grid shows ratings; we show them only when real.
- Charging more because a status signal exists: we do not suggest owners raise prices. Prices stay the owner's own.

## For our pages

1. The price is a promise. Put one plain, true sentence about why this costs what it costs beside the price or package (made by hand, number of hours, what goes in), not only in an About page.
2. Show the maker. A named person or a real photo of the work in the first screen, plus one real 3 to 8 second clip of the making with a single line over it.
3. Prove it with 2 to 4 big real figures (years, orders, events, sizes served) over one-line labels, or leave the row out.
4. Keep the product row calm. One even-lit photo per item on the same ground, same crop, same caption size; the premium feeling comes from restraint and sameness, not from effects.
5. Let the shopper see themselves. At least one photo on the home page of a real customer or real setting using the product (a table set with the board, a hand holding the cup), with permission.
6. Use the same line and the same photos on the link preview, the home page and the shop so the brand repeats instead of being re-explained.
