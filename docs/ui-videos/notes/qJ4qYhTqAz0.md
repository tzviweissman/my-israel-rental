## Lessons

1. **Fewer choices on the first screen.** A second, different kind of buy beside the main one (credits beside a subscription) turned one choice into a comparison and lost revenue. One main action per screen; a side option goes one tap away, not beside it. SAID and SHOWN 0:55-1:35. NEW (3l.4 compares similar items; it does not say to remove a rival option).
2. **Count the actions in the first viewport.** Checkable: in the first screen of each page and each order step, at most one primary button and at most one visible alternative (a plain text link). Fails when two solid buttons or two product types compete. Derived from lesson 1. NEW.
3. **The first option is read as the normal pick, so choose it on purpose.** Annual went from 60% to 75 to 80% when it led and monthly sat one tap away (presenter's figure). The size, package or service the owner most wants to sell goes first or in the middle, with a plain line of what it includes. SAID and SHOWN 1:45-2:20. NEW; sits beside 3j.6 (the middle option). No "Most popular" label unless the owner says so (3c.5).
4. **A real taste beats a smaller price.** The free trial made 140% more than a discounted intro price (company figure, a direction not a promise). A free taster, first session, sample box or free quote the owner truly offers goes ahead of a first-order discount. SAID and SHOWN 2:30-3:05. ALREADY 3j.3 (give first); the NEW part is the ranking: give-first before a first-order discount (3j.4) when the owner offers both.
5. **Never blur or hide the thing, then ask for payment.** The old Undertone wall half-blurred the user's own recaps; the new one shows everything and asks one question. No blurred menu, no gated price list, no "sign up to see prices". SHOWN 3:10-3:30. NEW as a check.
6. **Ask one plain question to set the package.** "How many sessions a month?" with a stepper replaces a menu of plans with one question and a price on the button. "How many people?" for a caterer, "How many boxes?" for a bakery. Real prices only. SHOWN 3:15-3:30. NEW as a replacement for a long list (the preset-package rule from the GGg61sdEjeI notes is the near neighbour).
7. **Show how a trial, deposit or first visit works, step by step.** A three-step timeline above the button (today, reminder, when anything is charged) means no surprise. For us only where there is a real trial, deposit or cancellation window, in the owner's terms: "Today you book. 48 hours before we confirm. Cancel free until then." SHOWN 1:55-2:15. NEW.
8. **Change structure before price.** When a page does not sell, change the order and number of options before touching prices or copy, one change at a time. SAID 3:40. NEW (process, not a page rule).
9. **Small, fast tests.** Pregnancy+ went from one test a quarter to three a month once tests were easy to ship. ALREADY (the review loop in 3a and 3r), no change.

## Do not copy

- The revenue numbers (14%, 60 to 75 to 80%, 140%) as promises or copy. They are second-hand claims from the presenter and none may appear on a client page.
- "Best value", "Save 20%" badges, "No charge for 14 days" and "Start free trial" wording unless the owner really offers exactly that.
- The agency pitch, the free strategy call plug, and the plug for his retention video.
- Blurred or gated content, and the "trick" framing (choice overload, default effect, endowment effect). The honest use is to make the real best option easy, not to hide the others.
- Subscription logic as such: small businesses rarely sell a recurring plan. Use the ideas for packages, sizes and bookings only.

## For our pages

- A bakery, coffee shop or caterer opens on one thing to do, "Order" or "Book", with the second option a quiet text link, not a second solid button.
- Put the owner's real best seller first or in the middle of the sizes and say plainly what it feeds or includes; the order is a decision, not an accident.
- A nail salon or gym leads with the first visit or class if the owner offers one, with a three-line "what happens next" under the button (book, confirmation, cancel rules). That reads as a real business.
- Replace a long package list with one question and a price on the button ("How many sessions a month? 2: the real price"), from the owner's own prices.
- Never hide prices or blur photos to push a sign-up. Showing everything openly is part of looking high-end and honest.
- Gift shops and meat boards: show the real saving in money on a bigger size or bundle (3j), and keep any alternative purchase off the main screen.
