## Candidate rules for us

Context: one-page sites for small local businesses in Israel, honest persuasion only. Most of this video is paid-ads D2C tactics and does not transfer; the useful core is congruence and specificity.

1. Congruence. The words, the audience and the offer on the page must match whatever sent the visitor there (an Instagram post, a Google listing, a flyer). One page, one promise. SAID, strongly (12:10-12:45, 15:30-16:10). Not shown as a layout; we apply it by making the hero headline name the specific service and audience.
2. Be specific about who it is for. Name the person and situation in the headline ("for new mums in Haifa", "catering for 40-120 guests") rather than a generic tagline. SAID (1:50-2:00, 4:28-4:50) and SHOWN in the Gruns headline.
3. Lead with benefits in plain words and keep proof near the headline: a small strip with real review count and rating, above the headline as in the Gruns frame. SHOWN (2:24). Honest version: only reviews and numbers the business really has, with a source we can show. If none exist, leave the strip out.
4. One primary button, repeated, with the specific offer on it and a short reassurance line directly under it ("Free quote, no obligation" only where true). SHOWN (2:24).
5. Name the real cause of the problem ("it's not your fault") where it is accurate. SAID (5:40, 9:00). No fear tactics about health or money.
6. Do the selling before the ask: for considered purchases a longer, informative page beats rushing the click. SAID (12:46-13:15). Supports sections for process, pricing and FAQ.
7. Show the real price and what is included in plain terms. A "price per day" framing is fine only with the total also visible. PARTLY SAID (6:22).
8. A short chooser or quiz for a business with several packages (a coach recommending a plan). SHOWN (9:44). Keep it to 3-5 questions, not the 30 shown, and never fake a "results" screen.

## Rejected for honesty

- Countdown timers and "limited time sale" banners (seen on Gruns at 2:24 and Create at 14:50): rejected, fake urgency unless the end date is real and fixed.
- Free gifts priced as "$80 of value" and slashed-through prices "to create the illusion of a deal" (6:10, 11:30): rejected, invented anchor prices. The presenter himself calls it fraud-y.
- Showing a monthly price while charging quarterly (6:32): rejected, misleading pricing.
- Sweepstakes "chance to win" hooks (11:50): rejected as a pressure device.
- Intro discount then full-price charge 30 days later with cancel-anytime (2:50): rejected for us; we do not run subscriptions. If a business has a trial, state the renewal price and date next to the button.
- 30-40 personas, 1,200 ads, celebrity and doctor endorsements: not applicable, and not allowed without real endorsements. Never invent "clinicians recommend" badges.
- Social-proof counts ("1M+ members", "100K+ reviews"): never invent; only real, verifiable ones.

## Reliability
The speaker is an ad marketer promoting a sponsor and says he has no view on whether the products work. Treat revenue and conversion claims (such as 70-80% quiz completion) as guesses; he says "would not be surprised", not measured.
