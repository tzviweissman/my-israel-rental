## The one transferable idea
Give the AI a real client brief plus the real assets, let it build the whole home page in the editor in one prompt, then reuse that finished page's "design language" as the instruction for every other page, in one prompt, with a written task list it ticks off. The brief states what the site must NOT do ("No prices, baskets, Add to bag, subscriptions or checkout; every buying action leads to Find a stockist") and the build keeps to that.

## What to discount
- Sponsored by Framer; the whole workflow is tied to a paid platform and its MCP. The "client can edit in a CMS" argument is the product pitch.
- No measurement of quality, speed, accessibility, performance, Hebrew/RTL, real mobile screenshots, or cost in money. The usage figure (10%) is unmeasured and unlabelled.
- Animation values (140 px, 1.2 s, 84-120%, 24-56 px) are Claude's own text, not something he measured, and Claude says it never watched the result. The motion on video is a few seconds of preview.
- Not shown: the first-pass result for the "homepage direct from brief" attempt versus the Figma one beyond one before/after board; how long "a few minutes" was; any fix-up rounds; the actual stockist data (sample, invented by the AI).
- The funnel at the end (code KYLE, futuredesigner.io).
- The assets (tin, beans, logo) were supplied ready-made; the video does not show how they were made.

## Ours? (rows we do NOT already do, and where they would go)
1. Claude's own "Not done yet" list at the end of each build (what the build does not do: breakpoints, links going nowhere, controls that look right but do nothing) and a written phase task list that ends in "screenshot-verify every page and breakpoint". We check with scripts (3r) but have no required "not done yet" report from the builder to the owner. Extends 3r and 4.
2. The brief states what the site must not do, in the client's own words ("no prices, baskets, checkout; every buying action leads to Find a stockist"). We have an order flow, so this applies as a per-business "what the buy button does" line in the brief. Extends section 2 (what the AI gets) and 3q (concept sheet).
3. Small tilted sticker label on every section headline, and a circular ring-text badge on the hero. 3t/3p cover look per category and unique touches, but there is no rule about a repeated label device per heading. Extends 3p (one unique touch) as a candidate, not a requirement.
4. Bean-style parallax with different speeds for blurred (far) versus sharp (near) copies, shadows tied to each piece's speed. Section 9 says no parallax on phones and no parallax for decoration; layered speed tied to blur could be a desktop-only option for hero floating objects. Extends 3h/3f effects, desktop only.
5. Hero load sequence with named order and numbers (nav, headline words, product rise 140 px over 1.2 s, text, card, badge last, 1.6 s total) and "plays once". Our film opening rules (3h) are scroll-driven so a timed load sequence applies only to non-film heroes (section 9 says the hero yields to a load sequence). Extends section 9.
6. Stockist/venue finder as a list with tags plus a stylised brand-coloured map with numbered pins that goes sticky beside the list. Only relevant to a business with several outlets. Extends 3b (category checklist) for a `place` category.
7. Design-in-Figma-first with an 8 px system, 14-inch frame, 12-column grid, 16 px gutter, then hand off to the builder. We use the starter instead; no grid spec exists in the rules. Extends 3c/3s (layout). Low value for us.
