# How To Scientifically Design Addictive Apps

- Channel: Tim Gabe (agency Sips App / Zipzap). URL: https://youtu.be/yBpv5rZoBjA. Length: about 15 min.
- Watched via 960px frames sampled every 6 s (all 13 contact sheets viewed) plus full-size frames of the key demos, aligned against the transcript. About half the runtime is the presenter talking to camera; the on-screen material is app screenshots (Finch, Duolingo, Freecash, Peloton, League of Legends, Strava) and typed-out bullet slides.

- 1:27 Claim (said, text slide with highlighted source): average app keeps about 7% of users at 30 days; the apps discussed reach up to 90% annual retention. No source for the 90% beyond Peloton.

Shown: a checklist of real self-care tasks with tick boxes; a location picker grid of 12 illustrated destinations (frame 3:24); the bird goes on an adventure with a "back in" timer; a personality screen with six progress bars (Security, Logic, Compassion, Confidence, Head Tilt Curiosity, Resilience, each "n/30", frame 4:18). Said: daily "discoveries" vary, some days great, some nothing; traits drift in ways you did not choose. He calls this a gentle variable-reward "craving machine".

- 5:45-6:40 Takeaways typed on slides: mostly predictable and transparent rewards, "controlled surprise", and one visible progress measure rather than 20 scattered badges.
- 6:41-7:54 Loss aversion. Shown: Duolingo streak "185"; Freecash streak cards: "You got your first 7-day streak!", "You just hit a 42-day streak!", "You reached an 86-day streak! ## Candidate rules for us
Honesty filter first: the video's thesis is compulsion by design. Most is rejected; a small honest core is kept.

1. One clear progress measure, not many badges. SAID 6:23, SHOWN (Finch bars 4:18; Freecash tiers 7:36-7:48). **Adopt in an honest form**: in a multi-step flow (order, booking, coach programme) show one plain progress element, e.g. "Step 2 of 4" or "Session 6 of 12", with true numbers only. Test: every progress number on a page maps to a real record.
2. Transparent next reward ("6 more days until..."). SHOWN 7:48. **Adopt in an honest form**: only when the business really runs a loyalty offer, e.g. "5th loaf free, you have 3"; show the real rule and real count.
3. One dominant high-contrast primary button on a reward or confirmation card (green "Claim Reward"). SHOWN 7:36. **Adopt** as a layout rule: one primary action per confirmation screen ("Reorder", "Add to calendar"); brand colours apply.
4. Milestone visuals that change by tier (gem colours). SHOWN 7:48. **Adopt in an honest form** for real milestones only, e.g. a coach client's page shows "12 weeks completed". Invented milestones rejected.
5. Show where you stand against a real total (Peloton "1 / 2,075"). SHOWN 8:30. **Adopt in an honest form** for the customer's own history (past orders, own log). Public rankings: **rejected**, social-comparison pressure, and Strava's cheating story (10:30) shows it corrupts data.
6. Variable/random rewards (Finch discoveries, LoL hidden win-rate balancing). SHOWN 3:24, SAID 5:08. **Rejected: random/variable rewards are on our banned list**, and LoL's is deliberate outcome manipulation.
7. Loss-aversion streaks and the red "Don't lose your streak" card with a recovery button. SHOWN 7:36. **Rejected: guilt streaks.** Neutral alternative: "Your saved order is ready to repeat", no loss framing.
8. Never-finish design: no done states, seasonal resets, uncapped metrics. SAID/SHOWN (LoL ladder). **Rejected: engineered endlessness.** Our opposite rule: every flow ends clearly ("Done, your order is confirmed") with a visible exit.
9. Public achievement and leaderboards (12:45). SAID. **Rejected** for small-business pages; no benefit to the customer.
10. Human relationship as the retention driver (Peloton instructors, 10:58). SAID. **Adopt**: show the owner/coach/cleaner by name with a real photo and a direct WhatsApp link; no stock faces, no invented testimonials.
11. Retention figures like "7% vs 90%" (1:27). SAID, thinly sourced. **Do not use** any such number in copy.
