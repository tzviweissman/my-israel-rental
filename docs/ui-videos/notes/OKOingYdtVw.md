## Candidate rules for us
Same honesty filter. This video is a catalogue of compulsion tactics, so most items are rejected. The loop is useful as a checklist of what to avoid, with honest cousins.

1. Variable rewards / not knowing what you will get (0:37). SHOWN as TikTok clips. **Rejected: banned random/variable rewards.**
2. Remove stopping cues, endless scroll (1:49). SHOWN (endless feed, break dialog 2:30). **Rejected: endless feeds.** Honest inverse, **adopt**: every list ends (finite menu, finite services list), a clear "Done" state after ordering, a visible way to leave.
3. The "Would you like to continue?" break dialog (2:30) is the thing addictive apps avoid. **Adopt as the opposite principle**: long forms get a summary and "Save and finish later"; never trap the user.
4. Zeigarnik / unfinished loops, unread badges, "mentioned you" nudges (3:18). SHOWN. **Adopt in an honest form**: contact someone only about a real unfinished thing they started (a saved cart, an unconfirmed booking, "Your order for Friday is not confirmed yet"), sent once, with an easy off switch. **Rejected**: fake unread counts or badges with nothing behind them.
5. Social reinforcement: likes, reactions, followers (3:56). SHOWN as phone mocks. **Rejected** for our pages. Verified real reviews via the emailed review link stay allowed; no invented ones.
6. Loss-aversion streaks, Snapchat flames (5:24). SHOWN. **Rejected: guilt streaks.**
7. Trigger-Action-Reward-Investment loop (6:00-7:00). SHOWN as cards. **Adopt in an honest form** as a design review checklist: Trigger = a real event (order ready, appointment tomorrow); Action = one tap; Reward = the actual service delivered; Investment = saved details that make the next order faster (address, favourite items, reorder button). Test: if a step exists only to extend session time, remove it.
8. Investment via the feed learning your likes (6:00). SAID/SHOWN. **Adopt in an honest form** as saved items and "order again" for returning customers, stored with consent and deletable.
9. Closing claim that these are harmless "if leveraged properly" (7:25). SAID only. **Not adopted** as a rule; unsupported.
