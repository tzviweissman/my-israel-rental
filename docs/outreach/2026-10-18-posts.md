- **Only where people are asking.** Look for posts asking for a restaurant, a rental, a caterer, a cleaner. Don't post the same text in ten groups on one day.

## 3. Times of Israel blog article

Apply to write at **blogs.timesofisrael.com** (the "Write for us" or "Become a blogger" link). Approval usually takes a few days. Their editors may change the title.

**Title:** Kosher restaurants in Jerusalem: how to know what you're relying on

**Article:**

Ask five people in Jerusalem whether a restaurant is kosher and you can get five answers. "It's kosher." "It's Rabbanut, but not mehadrin." "It was Badatz, I think they switched." "They're closed on Shabbat, so it must be fine." For someone new to the city, or visiting for a week, it's hard to know what any of that means, and harder to check.

Here is what I've learned about checking, and a tool I built because I couldn't find one that did it.

**The certificate is the answer, not the sign.** A restaurant in Israel that is supervised displays a kashrut certificate, a teudat kashrut. It names who supervises the place, whether it is meat, dairy or parve, and the dates it is valid for. A sticker in the window or the word "kosher" on the menu is not the same thing. Some places are "kosher-style": the food may contain no pork or shellfish, but nobody is supervising the kitchen.

**Know the level.** A local Rabbanut certificate is the basic level. Many restaurants also carry, or instead carry, a private hechsher, such as one of the badatz certifications, often described as mehadrin. Which one you rely on is your own decision, or your rav's. What matters is knowing which one you are looking at.

**Check the date.** Certificates are renewed regularly. A certificate that has expired doesn't necessarily mean anything is wrong, but it's worth asking.

**Meat or dairy matters beyond the menu.** A dairy restaurant may serve fish, and some people have their own practices about that. If you've eaten meat, a dairy restaurant may not work for you for some hours afterwards. The certificate tells you which kind of kitchen it is.

**Shabbat closing is a good sign, not proof.** Most supervised restaurants close on Shabbat, but closing on Shabbat doesn't make a place supervised.

When I tried to find kosher restaurants by neighbourhood with the hechsher listed, I ended up checking each restaurant's own website one at a time. So I built a list. It now has 5,324 kosher restaurants across Israel, 1,182 of them in Jerusalem, each listed with the hechsher it names on its own site, and whether it is meat or dairy. You can filter by city, neighbourhood and hechsher at myisraelrental.com/restaurants/jerusalem. It's free and there's nothing to sign up for.

It is a starting point, not a psak. Hechshers change, and a list can be out of date before anyone notices. When you get to the door, look at the certificate on the wall. If something on the list is wrong, there is a button to report it, and I fix it.

For anyone moving here or visiting: the certificate, the level, the date, meat or dairy. Those four answer most of the question.
